Compound: TBACl (Tetrabutylammonium chloride; C₁₆H₃₆ClN)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [CH₃(CH₂)₃]₄N⁺ · Cl⁻  
- Functional groups:  
  - **Tetrabutylammonium cation (TBA⁺):** bulky quaternary ammonium ion with four long C₄H₉ chains forming a hydrophobic “alkyl umbrella” that shields the perovskite surface from H₂O/O₂; the positive charge compensates negatively charged surface defects (e.g., halide vacancies).  
  - **Chloride anion (Cl⁻):** mobile halide species that can temporarily occupy I⁻ vacancies, aid crystal growth, and reduce trap states.  
- Structural behavior: due to its steric bulk, TBA⁺ cannot penetrate the perovskite lattice; instead, it forms a uniform surface and grain-boundary coating, improving film compactness, reducing defect density, and enhancing interfacial energy alignment.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Defect passivation and ion regulation:**  
  TBACl repairs halide vacancies (particularly I⁻ vacancies) via chemical reduction of I₂ to I₃⁻, suppressing halide loss and vacancy formation. The Cl⁻ anion temporarily fills halide sites, while the TBA⁺ cation electrostatically neutralizes surface negative defects. Together, they reduce deep-level traps and mitigate nonradiative recombination[1].  
- **Surface coating and grain-boundary healing:**  
  The bulky, hydrophobic TBA⁺ cations form a physical barrier over perovskite grains and interfaces, effectively “blanketing” grain boundaries to suppress defect-mediated recombination and inhibit ion migration. This coating also moderates crystal growth, yielding smoother and denser films.  
- **Interface engineering and heterostructure formation:**  
  In certain architectures (e.g., carbon-based, HTL-free perovskite solar cells), vapor or post-treatment with TBACl promotes the reaction of TBA⁺/Cl⁻ with residual PbI₂, generating a thin 2D/3D heterointerface. This interfacial layer passivates traps, enhances charge transfer, and blocks electron back-injection, thereby improving interfacial charge selectivity.  
- **Energy-level tuning and charge transport:**  
  The quaternary ammonium charge dipole slightly modifies the surface potential, optimizing band alignment at the perovskite/electrode interface and facilitating carrier extraction. The result is improved charge transport and reduced hysteresis.  
- **Stability enhancement:**  
  The hydrophobic TBA⁺ layer protects against UV-induced decomposition, moisture attack, and halide volatilization. Ion-pair formation (TBA⁺···Cl⁻) also immobilizes mobile halides, improving resistance to light, heat, and environmental stress[2].

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** enhanced through reduced surface recombination and improved interfacial band alignment.  
- **FF:** improved due to better carrier extraction and suppression of interfacial charge accumulation.  
- **Jsc:** typically maintained or slightly increased as the surface remains optically transparent and electronically benign.  
- **Stability:** significantly improved UV, moisture, and thermal tolerance owing to hydrophobic coverage and ion-migration suppression.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Main perovskite reflections** (e.g., (100)/(110)) remain intact, confirming that TBA⁺ does not intercalate into the lattice.  
- **Reduced PbI₂ (≈12.6°) intensity** indicating consumption or passivation of excess PbI₂.  
- **No additional impurity peaks** observed, consistent with surface-only modification.  
- Occasional weak **low-angle reflections (~4–6° 2θ)** suggest the formation of an ultrathin 2D perovskite-like interfacial layer.  
- Peak sharpening or FWHM narrowing indicates improved film uniformity and reduced structural disorder.
