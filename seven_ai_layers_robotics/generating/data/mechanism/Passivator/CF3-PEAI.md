Compound: CF₃-PEAI (Trifluoromethyl-phenylethylammonium iodide; C₉H₁₁F₃IN)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [CF₃C₆H₄–CH₂–CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · I⁻  
- Functional groups:  
  - **–NH₃⁺ (protonated primary amine):** forms electrostatic and hydrogen-bond interactions with surface halide vacancies and under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites, effectively passivating electronic traps.  
  - **Trifluoromethyl (CF₃):** strongly electron-withdrawing and hydrophobic, lowers surface energy, increases moisture/oxygen resistance, and tunes interfacial energy levels.  
  - **Phenyl ring (π-conjugated):** provides rigidity and a hydrophobic π barrier, contributing to interfacial energy-level alignment and reduced surface disorder.  
  - **Ethyl linker (–CH₂–CH₂–):** flexible spacer connecting the aromatic and ionic segments, allowing the ammonium head to access defect sites without significant lattice distortion.  
  - **I⁻ (iodide):** fills iodide vacancies, suppresses halide-ion migration, and mitigates nonradiative recombination.  
- Structural behavior: during post-treatment and mild annealing, CF₃-PEAI induces the formation of an ultrathin **(CF₃PEA)₂PbI₄ 2D layer**, establishing a graded 2D/3D heterojunction that passivates both surface and grain-boundary defects while building a hydrophobic surface barrier.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Defect passivation:**  
  The ammonium headgroup forms strong N–H···I hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions with surface halide vacancies (V_I⁻) and under-coordinated Pb²⁺. Meanwhile, iodide anions from CF₃-PEAI replenish halide vacancies and suppress ion migration. The CF₃ substituent enhances Pb²⁺ coordination and reduces trap density further through electron-withdrawing effects[1].  
- **Formation of 2D/3D gradient heterojunction:**  
  Upon spin-coating and annealing, CF₃-PEAI produces a thin (CF₃PEA)₂PbI₄ layer on top of the 3D perovskite. This 2D layer extends tens of nanometers into the surface, repairing near-surface and grain-boundary defects and enabling smooth band alignment between the 2D and 3D regions[1].  
- **Hydrophobic and energy-level modulation:**  
  The CF₃-terminated surface forms a low-surface-energy, hydrophobic interface that prevents moisture/oxygen penetration. The electron-withdrawing CF₃ also slightly downshifts the perovskite valence band, optimizing energy alignment with charge-transport layers and suppressing interfacial recombination[2].  
- **Combined effect:**  
  Through synergistic passivation, band alignment, and barrier formation, CF₃-PEAI simultaneously enhances Voc, improves FF, and substantially strengthens environmental and operational stability.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** significant increase owing to deep trap removal and optimized band alignment.  
- **FF:** improved due to reduced interfacial recombination and better charge extraction.  
- **Jsc:** largely retained or slightly increased due to preserved light absorption and efficient carrier transport.  
- **Stability:** markedly enhanced resistance to humidity, oxygen, and thermal stress, attributable to the hydrophobic CF₃ layer and suppressed ion migration.

3. Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **New low-angle reflections (~4–6° 2θ)** corresponding to the formation of (CF₃PEA)₂PbI₄ 2D perovskite phase.  
- **Main 3D perovskite peaks** remain unchanged, confirming intact bulk structure.  
- **FWHM narrowing** of the (100)/(110) peaks, indicating improved crystallinity and reduced lattice strain.  
- **PbI₂ (≈12.6°) peak intensity decreased**, reflecting consumption of excess PbI₂ and effective halide-vacancy passivation.  
- The resulting pattern supports formation of a coherent 2D/3D graded heterojunction without secondary impurity phases.
