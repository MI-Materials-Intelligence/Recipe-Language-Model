Compound: PbC₂O₄ (Lead(II) oxalate)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Chemical formula: PbC₂O₄ = Pb²⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻  
- Structural features:  
  - The oxalate anion (C₂O₄²⁻) acts as a **bidentate chelating ligand**, coordinating to Pb²⁺ via both carboxylate oxygens to form a five-membered Pb–O–C–C–O ring.  
  - In solid state, PbC₂O₄ forms **1D polymeric chains** where each oxalate bridges adjacent Pb²⁺ ions, generating a network stabilized by van der Waals and weak hydrogen-bond interactions.  
  - The **Pb²⁺ 6s² lone pair** induces slight off-centering distortion, giving local steric protection once the material coats the perovskite surface.  
- Electronic & interfacial behavior:  
  - Acts as a **Lewis-base passivator**, coordinating under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites and compensating surface charge imbalance.  
  - Chemically inert and water-insoluble (≈0.00065 g L⁻¹), forming a durable, hydrophobic, and impermeable shell layer on perovskite films.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Defect passivation and lattice healing:**  
  The C₂O₄²⁻ anion chelates under-coordinated Pb²⁺ centers at the perovskite surface and grain boundaries via bidentate –O–C(=O)–C(=O)–O– coordination, directly healing Pb²⁺ vacancies and antisite defects. This effectively removes deep-level traps, suppresses nonradiative recombination, and enhances carrier lifetime and Voc[1].  
- **Bridging grain boundaries:**  
  The oxalate ligand simultaneously binds multiple Pb²⁺ sites, acting as a molecular “suture” that connects neighboring grains, reduces grain-boundary recombination, and reinforces film cohesion.  
- **Charge compensation and potential stabilization:**  
  Each divalent oxalate balances excess positive charge arising from halide vacancies, reducing surface potential fluctuations and smoothing interfacial energy alignment for efficient charge extraction.  
- **Barrier and interfacial protection:**  
  The dense Pb–oxalate framework forms a chemically robust, water-insoluble surface layer that impedes H₂O and O₂ diffusion and blocks halide volatilization. This dual physical–chemical barrier protects the perovskite from environmental degradation and electrode corrosion.  
- **Phase stabilization and ion-migration suppression:**  
  Pb–oxalate coordination strengthens Pb–I lattice bonds, raises the formation energy of iodide vacancies, and inhibits the α→δ phase transition in FAPbI₃. It also prevents halide and Pb²⁺ migration under illumination and heat, enhancing long-term operational stability.  
- **Interfacial energy-level alignment:**  
  The oxalate layer subtly modifies the perovskite surface work function, improving energy-level matching with electron-transport layers (e.g., C₆₀, SnO₂) and reducing interfacial recombination losses.  
- **Synergistic passivation with other layers:**  
  When combined with Lewis-acidic ETLs such as PCBM or C₆₀, PbC₂O₄ serves as an interfacial buffer that complements ETL defect healing and enhances charge extraction at the perovskite/ETL junction.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** significantly enhanced due to deep-level trap elimination and suppressed surface recombination.  
- **FF:** improved via better charge extraction and reduced interfacial losses.  
- **Jsc:** largely maintained or slightly increased owing to improved film quality and reduced recombination pathways.  
- **Stability:** markedly enhanced against humidity, oxygen, UV, and thermal stress due to the insoluble Pb–oxalate surface barrier and immobilized halide ions.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Main 3D perovskite peaks** preserved, confirming that PbC₂O₄ forms primarily at the surface without disrupting the bulk lattice.  
- **Reduced PbI₂ (≈12.6°) peak intensity**, consistent with defect repair and Pb²⁺ coordination.  
- **Additional weak reflections** characteristic of PbC₂O₄ (≈18–22° 2θ) may appear, indicating surface-layer formation.  
- Narrowed FWHM of perovskite reflections suggests enhanced crystallinity and lower defect-induced strain.  
- No impurity or degradation peaks observed, confirming stable Pb–oxalate surface formation and coherent 2D/3D interface integration.
