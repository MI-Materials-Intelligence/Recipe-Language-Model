# Compound: 1,8-Octanediamine Dihydriodide  
*(ODADI; CAS No. 2044283-92-1; Molecular formula: C₈H₂₂I₂N₂)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **English name:** 1,8-Octanediamine dihydriodide  
- **Abbreviation:** ODADI  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₂₂I₂N₂  
- **Ionic representation:**  
  [H₃N⁺–(CH₂)₈–NH₃⁺]·2I⁻

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

### 2.1 Protonated Primary Amine (–NH₃⁺)
- **Cationic terminal:**  
  Electrostatic pairing with halide vacancies (e.g., I⁻ vacancies) on the perovskite surface, neutralizing local negative charge.
- **Hydrogen-bond donor:**  
  Forms N–H···I–Pb hydrogen bonds to passivate Pb²⁺ defects and reduce non-radiative recombination.
- **Chain-end anchoring:**  
  Dual –NH₃⁺ termini create a “molecular stitch” that bridges grains and suppresses ion migration.
- **Energy-level tuning:**  
  Ammonium dipoles adjust surface work function, optimizing charge extraction.

### 2.2 Iodide Ions (I⁻)
- **Charge compensation:**  
  Supply additional iodide to fill surface iodide vacancies, suppressing halide-vacancy-induced defect states.
- **Hydrogen-bond acceptor:**  
  Participate in N–H···I networks, enhancing mechanical and chemical stability of the passivation layer.
- **Ion channel behavior:**  
  Excess I⁻ can volatilize or diffuse during annealing, assisting grain growth and reducing grain-boundary density.

---

## 3. Properties and Roles of ODADI

ODADI is a long-chain alkyl diammonium salt employed for precursor conditioning and interfacial engineering in perovskite solar cells.

### 3.1 Crystallization Control and Ion Diffusion
- In two-step processes, a pre-deposited ODADI layer interacts with PbI₂.  
- Promotes diffusion of organic halides within the PbI₂ film.  
- Regulates perovskite crystallization, yielding more uniform and controllable grain formation.

### 3.2 Defect Passivation
- Effectively passivates buried-interface defects and hole traps.  
- Suppresses non-radiative recombination and improves electronic properties and open-circuit voltage.  
- Passivation strength correlates with alkyl-chain length, with longer chains providing stronger effects.

### 3.3 Suppression of Intermediates and Residual By-products
- –NH₃⁺ groups interact with halides, reducing formation of the PbI₂–DMSO intermediate[1].  
- Lowers residual PbI₂/PbBr₂ content.  
- Enables a more orderly crystallization pathway and mitigates film instability associated with persistent intermediates.

### 3.4 Improved Film Quality
- Produces films with larger grains and smoother surfaces.  
- Reduces trap-state density, indicating enhanced crystallinity.

### 3.5 Interfacial and Electrical Optimization
- Decreases series resistance (Rₛ).  
- Increases recombination resistance (R_rec).  
- Indicates reduced non-radiative recombination and improved charge transport.

---

## 4. Defect Passivation Mechanism

- **Cationic defect passivation:**  
  Terminal –NH₃⁺ groups form ionic and hydrogen bonds with under-coordinated Pb²⁺ at surfaces or grain boundaries, passivating deep-level defects[2].
- **Halide-vacancy healing:**  
  Released I⁻ fills iodine vacancies (V_I), reducing recombination centers associated with halide deficiencies.

---

## 5. Crystallization and Morphology Control

- Pre-deposited ODADI functions as a crystallization regulator.  
- Promotes organic-salt diffusion and induces dense, pinhole-free perovskite films with larger grains.  
- In wide-bandgap systems, suppresses intermediate-phase formation and improves crystal-orientation uniformity.

---

## 6. Interfacial Energy-Level and Charge-Transport Optimization

- Surface dipole tuning lowers interfacial barriers.  
- Enhances open-circuit voltage (Voc) and fill factor (FF).  
- Improves energy-level alignment with charge-transport layers, increasing carrier-extraction efficiency.
