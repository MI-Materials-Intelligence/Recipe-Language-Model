Compound: m-F-PEAI (meta-fluoro-phenylethylammonium iodide; C₈H₁₁FIN)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [3-F-C₆H₄–CH₂–CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · I⁻  
- Functional groups:  
  - **–NH₃⁺ (protonated primary amine):** anchors electrostatically to under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies, forming N–H···I–Pb hydrogen-bond networks that passivate deep-level defects.  
  - **Meta-fluorine (–F substituent):** electron-withdrawing, enhances π–π stacking for compact molecular packing; forms weak F···I hydrogen bonds that suppress I⁻ migration and reduce J–V hysteresis; improves film hydrophobicity and chemical stability.  
  - **Phenyl–ethyl chain:** provides a hydrophobic, flexible spacer that blocks moisture and buffers thermal strain, reducing microcrack formation.  
  - **I⁻ (iodide):** fills I⁻ vacancies and cooperates with –NH₃⁺ through N–H···I bonding to stabilize the lattice.  
- Structural behavior: m-F-PEAI induces in-situ formation of a thin **(m-F-PEA)₂PbI₄ 2D layer** on the 3D perovskite surface, creating a graded 2D/3D heterostructure that passivates defects, enhances energy-level alignment, and builds a hydrophobic, ion-locking interfacial barrier.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Dual-site defect passivation:**  
  –NH₃⁺ electrostatically binds under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies, forming stable N–H···I–Pb interactions that eliminate deep traps. The meta-fluorine substituent introduces additional weak F···I hydrogen bonds, effectively “locking” mobile iodides and suppressing ion migration.  
- **2D/3D interface engineering:**  
  Surface treatment with m-F-PEAI yields an ultrathin (m-F-PEA)₂PbI₄ layer atop the 3D perovskite. This 2D layer establishes an energy-level gradient, facilitating efficient charge extraction while blocking interfacial electron backflow. The graded junction enhances Voc and FF through reduced recombination losses[1].  
- **Crystallization and morphology optimization:**  
  m-F-PEAI moderates nucleation and crystal growth kinetics, promoting the formation of large-grain, dense perovskite films with fewer grain boundaries. The resulting smoother surface ensures improved contact with transport layers and lower trap-assisted recombination.  
- **Ion-migration suppression:**  
  The cooperative F···I hydrogen-bond network and the anchoring effect of –NH₃⁺ effectively pin mobile ions, minimizing ion drift under electric fields and significantly reducing hysteresis.  
- **Stability enhancement:**  
  The hydrophobic phenyl–F surface blocks water and oxygen infiltration, improving moisture and light stability. The molecular rigidity and strong interfacial packing protect the perovskite under thermal and operational stress.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** markedly improved due to deep-defect passivation and better interfacial energy alignment.  
- **FF:** enhanced through reduced interfacial recombination and suppressed hysteresis.  
- **Jsc:** maintained or slightly increased from improved crystallinity and smoother charge transport.  
- **Stability:** strongly enhanced under moisture, light, and thermal stress owing to hydrophobic fluorinated barrier and ion-migration suppression.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- Emergence of **low-angle reflections (~4–6° 2θ)** indicating the presence of (m-F-PEA)₂PbI₄ 2D perovskite phase atop the 3D lattice.  
- **Main 3D perovskite peaks** remain dominant and sharp, with **FWHM narrowing** of (100)/(110) reflections, showing improved crystallinity and reduced strain.  
- **PbI₂ (≈12.6°) peak intensity reduced**, consistent with halide-vacancy healing and passivation of residual PbI₂.  
- Absence of new impurity phases confirms coherent 2D/3D integration and stable interfacial modification.
