Compound: 5ATT (5-amino-1,3,4-thiadiazole-2-thiol; C₂H₃N₃S₂)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Structural skeleton: a 1,3,4-thiadiazole heterocycle bearing both **–SH (thiol)** and **–NH₂ (amine)** groups.  
- Functional characteristics:  
  - The **thiol (–SH)** and **ring nitrogen atoms** act as soft Lewis-base donors capable of forming strong coordination bonds with metal cations (Pb²⁺, Cu²⁺, Ag⁺, etc.), effectively anchoring to under-coordinated metal sites on perovskite surfaces.  
  - The **amino (–NH₂)** group serves as a hydrogen-bond donor toward halide anions (e.g., I⁻), enabling simultaneous cationic and anionic defect passivation.  
  - The conjugated thiadiazole ring enhances charge delocalization and stabilizes the metal–ligand bond network, contributing to durable surface modification.  
- Overall behavior: the bifunctional nature of 5ATT allows **Pb²⁺ coordination** via the thiol/nitrogen donors and **hydrogen-bonding** with halide vacancies, achieving dual-site chemical passivation at the perovskite interface.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Pb²⁺ defect passivation (chemical coordination):**  
  The thiol group (–SH) donates electrons to under-coordinated Pb²⁺, forming stable Pb–S bonds that neutralize metallic Pb clusters and Pb-vacancy defects. Meanwhile, the adjacent thiadiazole nitrogen atoms provide secondary coordination sites, forming four- or five-membered Pb–S–N chelate rings that reinforce the Pb–ligand linkage and eliminate deep-level trap states.  
- **Halide-vacancy passivation (hydrogen bonding):**  
  The amino group (–NH₂) at C-5 interacts with nearby iodide ions or vacancies through N–H···I⁻ hydrogen bonding. This interaction stabilizes the local halide framework, reduces iodide migration under bias, and strengthens lattice integrity[1].  
- **Dual-site cooperative passivation:**  
  The coexistence of soft Pb–S coordination and N–H···I hydrogen bonding provides synergistic suppression of both cationic and anionic defects, thereby reducing nonradiative recombination and improving interfacial charge transfer[2].  
- **Interfacial chemical stabilization:**  
  The 5ATT layer serves as a chemical shield that blocks perovskite–metal reactions, such as Pb reduction or electrode corrosion. Its sulfur-rich framework also scavenges reactive oxygen species, enhancing device operational longevity.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** significantly enhanced due to deep-trap removal and reduced surface recombination.  
- **FF:** improved via better interfacial contact and reduced carrier recombination losses.  
- **Jsc:** maintained or slightly increased due to improved charge extraction and suppressed interfacial resistance.  
- **Stability:** markedly improved against air, moisture, and light exposure, owing to the chemically bonded Pb–S network and hydrogen-bond reinforcement of the perovskite lattice.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Main 3D perovskite reflections** remain unchanged, indicating that 5ATT interacts at the surface rather than altering the bulk lattice.  
- **Reduced PbI₂ (≈12.6°) intensity**, consistent with chemical consumption of surface PbI₂ through Pb–S coordination.  
- **No additional impurity peaks**, confirming clean surface modification.  
- Possible slight **FWHM narrowing** of (100)/(110) peaks, reflecting improved crystallinity and reduced structural disorder after defect passivation.
