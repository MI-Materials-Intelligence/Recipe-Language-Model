## Spin Coating Speed PVK 2
### Control over solvent removal and thickness
Spin step 2 is where **thickness is largely set** and **solvent is rapidly removed**:
1. **Too low speed (~4000 rpm)**
- Centrifugal force is insufficient to remove most of the solvent; the wet film remains thick and “soft”.
- After spin-stop, lateral solvent flow continues, leading to thickness gradients and local dewetting.
- Film tends to be thick (dark) but rough, with micro-voids and intergranular cracks. Mean gray level is often in the **60–70** range, with strong local contrast.
2. **Optimal speed (~5000 rpm)**
- Solvent is removed quickly enough to freeze a uniform thin film, but not so fast that nucleation is explosive.
- When synchronized with the antisolvent pulse, the film transitions from a solvent-rich host to a perovskite-rich solid in a fairly uniform way.
- Coverage can be **>90–100%**, gray level typically **0–40** or **40–65** (compact dark film).
3. **Too high speed (>6000 rpm)**
- The wet film becomes ultra-thin before nucleation; any small perturbation or local wettability difference induces dewetting and bare spots.
- Evaporation is so fast that crystallization is highly kinetically limited, leading to small grains, high defect density, and macro-cracking.
- Effective coverage can fall to **0–30%**, with very bright regions (gray 90–120).
### 3Device metrics
- **Jsc**
- Under-thinning (low PVK2) → high thickness but high scattering and recombination at rough interfaces; Jsc is not maximized.
- Over-thinning (very high PVK2) → too thin/partial film with poor absorption; Jsc collapses.
- Optimal PVK2 gives a thickness that is near the absorption length for the bandgap and yields low-loss extraction, so Jsc is maximal.
- **Voc & FF**
- Over-rapid solvent removal (high PVK2) correlates with lots of micro-voids and trap states, which increase nonradiative recombination and thus lower Voc.
- Both under-thinning and over-thinning can produce local cracks and pinholes, which lower Rsh and degrade FF.
The optimal PVK2 speed is exactly where your images show a **dark, continuous film (gray 0–40, coverage close to 100%)**.
