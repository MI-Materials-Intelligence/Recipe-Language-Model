## Annealed Time PVK
### Kinetics of phase conversion and defect annealing
At a fixed temperature, **annealing time controls how far the film travels along the crystallization and defect-annealing pathway**:
1. **Too short time (e.g. 20 min at ~100 °C)**
- Adducts and intermediate phases are not fully converted; the α-phase network is incomplete.
- Many shallow and deep traps remain at grain boundaries and interfaces.
- Images: brighter average appearance (gray **~110**), strongly suggesting under-crystallized regions.
2. **Optimal time (e.g. 25–30 min at ~100–110 °C, or 45–60 min at exactly 100 °C depending on composition)**
- Grain growth proceeds until most grain boundaries are “healed” or reconfigured into lower-energy facets.
- Trap densities drop, and carrier diffusion length increases.
- Gray level progresses from **~55–60** (brown) to **~36** (dark compact film); coverage remains high.
3. **Too long time (>45–60 min, or very long at slightly higher T)**
- After grains reach an energetic optimum, further annealing promotes degradation pathways: halide loss, cation migration, secondary phase formation.
- Morphology: increased roughness, precipitated bright spots (PbI₂-rich), and sometimes micro-cracks. Gray level typically climbs again into the **50–70** range with more heterogeneous texture.
### Device metrics
- **Jsc**
- Too short → low crystallinity and traps → limited diffusion length, thus lower Jsc.
- Optimal → longest diffusion length and efficient extraction → highest Jsc.
- Too long → decomposition at surfaces/interfaces competes with transport; Jsc declines.
- **Voc & FF**
- Under-annealed films: high trap density → strong nonradiative recombination → low Voc and FF.
- Optimally annealed films: reduced trapping and improved mobility → Voc and FF are maximized.
- Over-annealed films: additional defect formation and roughness increase Rs and reduce Rsh, leading to lower FF and Voc despite initial grain growth benefits.
- **PCE**
- Shows a clear “volcano” dependence on annealing time at a given temperature, with an optimum where **coverage is near 100%, mean gray level is low (0–40), and phase purity plus grain size are both favorable**.
