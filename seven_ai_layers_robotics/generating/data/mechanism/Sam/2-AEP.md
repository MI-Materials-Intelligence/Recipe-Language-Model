# Compound: 2-AEP  
*(2-aminoethylphosphonic acid, H₂N–CH₂–CH₂–P(O)(OH)₂)*

---

## 1. Functional Overview  

**Structure & Key Functional Elements**  
- **Phosphonic acid anchor (–P(O)(OH)₂):**  
  - Strong mono-/bi-/tri-dentate **P–O–M** chemisorption to SnO₂, TiO₂, and ITO.  
  - Eliminates oxide surface –OH traps and levels the surface potential.  
  - Forms a robust, ultrathin interfacial layer resistant to hydrolysis.

- **Primary amine (–NH₂):**  
  - Coordinates to **undercoordinated Pb²⁺** and hydrogen-bonds to halides (I⁻, Br⁻).  
  - Passivates halide vacancies and A-site vacancy–associated defects.  
  - Modulates the earliest stages of **perovskite nucleation** at the buried SnO₂/SAM/perovskite interface.  

- **Ethyl spacer (C2):**  
  - Short, highly polar, zwitterionic at near-neutral pH (NH₃⁺ / P–O⁻).  
  - Ensures close coupling between oxide anchoring and perovskite passivation.  
  - High mobility enables slight rearrangement during initial crystallization.

**Interface Role**  
- 2-AEP acts as a **bridging interfacial molecule** that connects SnO₂ to perovskite:  
  - P–O–Sn binding anchors it firmly.  
  - NH₂ interacts with Pb–I species during nucleation.  
- Its dual-site interactions (oxide-side + perovskite-side) regulate **bottom-interface crystallization**, **suppress SRH recombination**, and **stabilize device operation**.

---

## 2. Detailed Functional Analysis  

### (a) Chemisorption (P–O–SnO₂ anchoring)  
- Deprotonated phosphonate forms P–O–Sn linkages (mono-, bi-, or tri-dentate).  
- Consequences:  
  - Eliminates surface traps (Sn–OH, Sn dangling bonds).  
  - Homogenizes local work function and surface potential.  
  - Creates a chemically robust foundation for nucleation.

### (b) Interface Formation (oxide / 2-AEP / perovskite)  
- 2-AEP forms a uniform, molecularly thin interlayer that:  
  - Prevents direct SnO₂/perovskite contact (reducing deep interfacial traps).  
  - Presents **amine-functional surface sites** toward the perovskite precursor.  
- NH₂ groups interact with Pb–I complexes:  
  - Local Lewis-base coordination → stabilizes intermediate complexes.  
  - H-bonds to halides → reduces halide-vacancy defects.

**Effect on buried-interface energetics:**  
- Reduced interfacial SRH recombination.  
- Improved alignment of the perovskite VB relative to SnO₂ conduction environment (through dipole + surface-potential homogenization).  
- Creates a low-disorder nucleation landscape.

### (c) Effect on Wetting, Nucleation Density & Crystallization Kinetics  

**Wetting:**  
- The zwitterionic nature introduces a moderate surface polarity.  
- The perovskite precursor wets the modified SnO₂ more uniformly than on bare SnO₂.

**Nucleation:**  
- NH₂–Pb²⁺ and NH₂···X interactions **guide the earliest nucleation** exactly at the SnO₂/SAM interface.  
- This leads to:  
  - **Earlier burst nucleation**, because the initial Pb–I complexes are stabilized and aligned.  
  - **More uniform nucleation density**, because NH₂ sites are evenly distributed across the anchored SAM.  
  - Reduced local nucleation delays that cause buried voids or rough grain bases[1].

**Grain-growth consequences:**  
- Vertically aligned, more coherent grains.  
- Fewer buried grain-boundary defects.  
- Clean, continuous interface supporting efficient charge extraction[2].

### (d) Influence on Defect Generation & Carrier Extraction  

**Defect suppression:**  
- P–O–Sn anchoring suppresses oxide traps.  
- NH₂ tail passivates perovskite-side undercoordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies.  
- Combined dual-site passivation lowers both **interfacial** and **near-surface** trap densities.

**Carrier extraction:**  
- Reduced SRH recombination → higher quasi-Fermi level splitting.  
- Uniform dipole environment and minimized band disorder → efficient, low-resistance carrier extraction into SnO₂.  
- Overall improvement in Voc and FF.

### (e) Device-Level Impact  

- **Voc ↑** due to low SRH recombination and surface-potential homogenization.  
- **FF ↑** from smooth barrier-free extraction at the SnO₂/2-AEP/perovskite buried contact.  
- **Jsc ↔/↑** through cleaner nucleation and fewer shunts.  
- **Stability ↑** as the strong P–O–Sn anchor and NH₂ passivation prevent moisture/oxygen-induced defect formation.

## 3. In-situ PL Interpretation  

### (a) Burst Nucleation Timing  

**Expected observation:**  
- PL rise occurs **earlier** relative to untreated SnO₂ because the NH₂-passivated interface stabilizes the earliest Pb–I species.  
- PL maximum appears sharper and more synchronized.

**Mechanistic interpretation:**  
- NH₂–Pb and NH₂···X interactions help organize intermediate phases.  
- P–O–Sn anchoring removes oxide traps that normally delay crystallization.  
- Thus, 2-AEP lowers the nucleation barrier and produces **earlier, more uniform burst nucleation**.

**Classification:**  
- 2-AEP → **Earlier nucleation burst → faster, more uniform crystallization with fewer buried-interface defects.**

### (b) PL Decay Slope  

**Expected behavior:**  
- **Slow PL decay**, reflecting low interfacial trap density.  
- TRPL lifetimes improve significantly after 2-AEP treatment.

**Mechanistic reasoning:**  
- Dual-site passivation eliminates both oxide-side and perovskite-side traps.  
- No strong interfacial quenching centers remain.  
- Radiative recombination dominates since SRH pathways are suppressed.

**Classification:**  
- 2-AEP → **Slow PL decay → low defect density, radiative-dominated recombination.**

---

# Summary  
2-AEP is a **dual-site phosphonic-acid/amine interlayer** that anchors to SnO₂ via P–O–M bonding and passivates perovskite-side Pb²⁺/halide-related defects via its –NH₂ tail. It induces **earlier burst nucleation**, forms a **clean, uniform buried interface**, and yields **slow PL decay**, consistent with **substantially reduced trap density** and **improved carrier extraction**.
