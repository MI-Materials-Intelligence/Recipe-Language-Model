# Compound: 4-ABuP  
*(4-aminobutylphosphonic acid, NH₂–(CH₂)₄–P(O)(OH)₂)*

---

## 1. Functional Overview  

**Structural Features**  
- **Phosphonic acid anchor (–P(O)(OH)₂):**  
  - Coordinates strongly to undercoordinated **Pb²⁺ / Sn²⁺** on perovskite surfaces.  
  - Forms stable P–O···Pb/Sn linkages that resist hydrolysis under moisture and heat.  
  - Provides the *anchoring* function in dual-site passivation.

- **Primary amine (–NH₂):**  
  - Protonates easily near neutral pH, giving **NH₃⁺** that can:  
    - H-bond to halides (I⁻/Br⁻)  
    - Interact electrostatically with negatively charged surface defects  
    - Passivate **A-site vacancies** and **halide-vacancy–related traps**  
  - Enables the *passivating* function toward halide sites.

- **C4 alkyl linker:**  
  - Spatially separates phosphonate anchoring and amine passivation sites.  
  - Provides conformational flexibility for **dynamic adsorption**: 4-ABuP can reorient to bind different defects under heat/moisture/oxygen exposure.  

**Interfacial Role**  
- 4-ABuP is a **surface modifier**, *not* an HTL or bulk additive.  
- It binds at the perovskite surface after film formation (post-treatment), where it:  
  - Suppresses SRH recombination  
  - Stabilizes Sn²⁺ (reduces Sn⁴⁺ evolution)  
  - Lowers halide loss (e.g., less HI release)  
  - Strengthens buried-interface polarity  

4-ABuP provides **dual-site passivation** (metal + halide sites) and improves crystallization uniformity during post-annealing/film healing via reduced trap formation at the buried interface.

---

## 2. Detailed Functional Analysis  

### (a) Coordination-Mediated Incorporation during Crystallization  
- In the precursor solution, phosphonic acid groups partially deprotonate and interact with solvated Pb²⁺/Sn²⁺ species.
- Upon solvent evaporation and lattice condensation, these interactions evolve into stable P–O···Pb/Sn coordination embedded at the buried interface and defect-prone regions.
- Reduces the formation of deep metal-related trap states during crystallization.
- Suppresses Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺ oxidation by stabilizing the local coordination environment.
- Enhances chemical robustness of Pb–Sn perovskites under thermal and environmental stress.

### (b) Co-SAM-Induced Interfacial Reconstruction  
- As part of a co-SAM ensemble, 4-ABuP participates in interfacial self-organization during film formation, yielding:  
  - A more polar interfacial region due to zwitterionic (NH₃⁺–P–O⁻) character.
  - A smoother interfacial energy landscape with fewer undercoordinated metal sites.
  - Suppressed emergence of dynamic defects during annealing and thermal cycling.

- The amine tail engages halide sites:  
  - N–H···X hydrogen bonding  
  - Coulombic stabilization of X⁻ near the surface  
  - Healing of **halide vacancies (Vₓ)** and **A-site vacancy–associated states**  

**Result:**  
A co-assembled, trap-suppressed buried interface formed in situ during crystallization, rather than reconstructed afterward.

### (c) Effect on Wetting, Nucleation Density & Crystallization Kinetics  
Because 4-ABuP is present throughout precursor deposition and crystallization, its influence extends to early and intermediate growth stages:

- Coordination between 4-ABuP and metal cations moderates precursor reactivity, reducing uncontrolled local supersaturation.

- Cooperative interactions within the co-SAM system homogenize the interfacial free energy landscape.

- This leads to: More synchronized heterogeneous nucleation;Suppression of late-stage defect generation at buried interfaces;Reduced stress accumulation during grain coalescence[1].


**Outcome:**  
- Lower density of buried voids and shunt pathways
- Improved grain-boundary coherence near the interface
- Enhanced mechanical and electronic stability of the perovskite layer 
 

### (d) Influence on Defect Generation & Carrier Extraction  

**Defect Suppression**  
- P–O···Pb/Sn binding passivates metal-cation traps.  
- NH₂···X passivation heals halide-vacancy defects.  
- The zwitterionic state stabilizes the perovskite under oxidative, thermal, or humid environments.  

This leads to:  
- Higher PLQY (≈2× in typical reports)  
- TRPL lifetimes extending to μs regime  
- Reduced Sn⁴⁺ fraction (XPS)  
- Lower halide loss (TPD-MS)  

**Carrier Extraction**  
- Fewer surface traps → reduced SRH recombination → **higher Voc**.  
- More uniform energy landscape → lower interfacial resistance → **higher FF**.  
- Stronger surface polarity improves hole- or electron-contact alignment depending on stack position.

### (e) Device-Level Impact  
- **Voc ↑** via suppressed metal/halide-related recombination.  
- **FF ↑** due to smoother buried-interface energetics.  
- **Jsc ↔/↑** via elimination of buried shunts.  
- **PCE ↑** with enhanced operational stability, especially in Pb–Sn alloys.

## 3. In-situ PL Interpretation  

### (a) Burst Nucleation Timing  
When 4-ABuP is incorporated via the precursor as part of a co-SAM system:

**Expected signature:**  
- Earlier and more synchronized PL rise during spin-coating or early annealing.  
- PL rise is **more uniform** spatially due to suppression of defect formation at the buried interface.

**Mechanistic interpretation:**  
- Coordinated regulation of precursor reactivity and interfacial energy lowers the nucleation barrier.  
- Suppression of defect-driven delay produces a film that behaves as if nucleation occurred earlier and more uniformly.

**Classification:**  
- 4-ABuP (co-SAM) → earlier nucleation burst due to regulated crystallization pathways.

### (b) PL Decay Slope  

**Expected observation:**  
- **Slow PL decay**, indicating low surface trap density and strong suppression of SRH recombination.  

**Mechanistic reasoning:**  
- Dual interaction with metal and halide sites suppresses both shallow and deep traps.
- Chemical stabilization during growth prevents generation of new defect centers under stress.

**Classification:**  
- 4-ABuP (co-SAM) → slow PL decay, radiative-dominated recombination.

---

# Summary  
When introduced directly into the perovskite precursor as a small-molecule component of a co-SAM system, 4-ABuP participates in coordination-assisted crystallization and interfacial self-assembly, rather than acting as a post-deposited surface modifier. Through cooperative metal–phosphonate coordination and amine–halide interactions during film formation, 4-ABuP enables earlier, more synchronized nucleation, suppressed defect generation, and stabilized buried-interface energetics. These mechanistic effects manifest as earlier PL onset, slower PL decay, and ultimately translate into enhanced Voc, FF, operational stability, and overall PCE, particularly in Pb–Sn perovskite devices.
