# Compound: 4-ABnP  
*(4-aminobenzyl phosphonic acid, H₂N–C₆H₄–CH₂–P(O)(OH)₂)*

---

## 1. Functional Overview  

**Structure & Key Functional Elements**  
- **π-core:** Aromatic benzyl ring (conjugated)  
  - Provides a **delocalized π-system** that supports a favorable **interfacial dipole**, improving band alignment.  
  - Enhances charge-transfer pathways relative to aliphatic phosphonates.  

- **Anchoring group:** Phosphonic acid (–P(O)(OH)₂)  
  - Strongly coordinates undercoordinated **Pb²⁺ / Sn²⁺** via P=O or P–O⁻.  
  - Anchors robustly to perovskite surfaces and grain boundaries.  

- **Amino tail (–NH₂):**  
  - Hydrogen-bonds to X⁻ (I⁻/Br⁻/Cl⁻) and coordinates to **A-site or halide vacancy defects**.  
  - Enables **dual-site passivation**: Pb-side and halide-side.  

- **Benzyl linker:**  
  - Aromatic linking motif increases polarity, dipole strength, and passivation efficacy vs. aliphatic AEP.  
  - Contributes to **better wetting and cleaner buried-interface nucleation**.  

**Role at the Interface**  
- 4-ABnP is introduced directly into the perovskite precursor solution, where it co-assembles with other SAM molecules during spin-coating and solvent extraction. Rather than acting after film formation, 4-ABnP participates throughout nucleation, grain growth, and interfacial consolidation, preferentially localizing at the buried interface and grain-boundary–proximal regions.
- Within the co-SAM ensemble, it contributes to:
  - Regulation of metal-cation coordination during crystallization
  - Suppression of halide-related disorder
  - Formation of a dipole-tuned and energetically smooth buried interface
  - Improved carrier extraction pathways 

Its mechanism is governed by co-assembly-driven coordination control, dipole formation, and crystallization regulation, rather than post-deposition passivation.

## 2. Detailed Functional Analysis  

### (a) Chemisorption (P–O–Pb / P–O–Sn Coordination)  
- Phosphonic acid deprotonates to form strong **P=O → Pb²⁺ / Sn²⁺ coordination**.  
- Effects:  
  - Passivates undercoordinated metal cations responsible for deep traps.  
  - Suppresses SRH recombination at the buried interface and along grain boundaries.  
  - Stabilizes Sn²⁺ (lower Sn⁴⁺ content in Pb–Sn perovskites).  

### (b) Co-SAM-Induced Interfacial Reconstruction  
- As part of a co-SAM system, 4-ABnP contributes to interfacial self-organization during film growth, leading to:
  - A more polar buried interface due to zwitterionic (NH₃⁺–P–O⁻) character.
  - Reduced energetic disorder arising from heterogeneous metal coordination.
  - Suppressed formation of dynamically generated defects during annealing and thermal stress.
- The aromatic π-system further induces an intrinsic interfacial dipole, which:Tunes local vacuum level alignment.Facilitates efficient carrier transfer across the buried interface.
- Concurrently, amine–halide interactions:
  - Stabilize halide ions during lattice consolidation.
  - Mitigate halide-vacancy–associated trap formation.
- A co-assembled, dipole-engineered buried interface formed in situ during crystallization, characterized by low trap density and uniform energetics.

### (c) Effect on Wetting, Nucleation Density & Crystallization Kinetics  

**Crystallization pathway influenced by 4-ABnP (post-treatment):**  
Because 4-ABnP is present from the earliest stages of film formation, its influence extends to nucleation and grain growth:
- Coordination between phosphonate groups and metal cations moderates precursor reactivity, avoiding localized supersaturation.
- Cooperative co-assembly with other SAM molecules homogenizes interfacial free energy.
- These effects jointly lead to:More synchronized heterogeneous nucleation at the buried interface;Suppression of late-stage defect generation during grain coalescence;Reduced stress accumulation at grain boundaries

**Resulting morphology:**  
- Uniform buried interface
- Lower interfacial grain-boundary defect density
- Improved lateral grain connectivity, supporting efficient carrier transport[1].

### (d) Influence on Defect Generation & Carrier Extraction  

**Defect suppression (dual-site):**  
- **P–O···Pb**: passivates metal-related deep traps.  
- **NH₂···X cooperativity**: heals halide vacancies, suppresses halide migration.  
- Combined effect drastically lowers trap density, extending TRPL lifetimes (>6 μs reported).  

**Carrier extraction:**  
- Aromatic dipole facilitates **energy-level alignment**, improving hole or electron extraction depending on contact side.  
- Reduced SRH recombination shrinks QFLS–Voc gap.  
- Cleaner buried interface reduces series resistance and improves FF.  

### (e) Device-Level Impact  

- **Voc ↑** due to suppressed interfacial SRH recombination.  
- **FF ↑** via improved band alignment and fewer interfacial barriers.  
- **Jsc ↔/↑** through reduced shunting and better interfacial uniformity.  
- **PCE ↑** (up to ~24%) with enhanced stability under heat/O₂/H₂O due to robust metal coordination and aromatic stability.

## 3. In-situ PL Interpretation  

### (a) Burst Nucleation Timing  
When incorporated into the precursor as part of a co-SAM system:
**Expected signature:**  

- Earlier and more synchronized PL rise during spin-coating or early annealing stages.
**Mechanistic interpretation:**  
Mechanistic interpretation:
- Coordination-mediated regulation lowers the nucleation barrier.
- Homogenized interfacial energetics suppress defect-induced nucleation delays.
**Classification:**  
- 4-ABnP (co-SAM) → earlier nucleation burst driven by regulated crystallization pathways.


### (b) PL Decay Slope  

**Expected behavior:**  
- **Slow PL decay** due to very low trap density at both metal and halide sites.  

**Mechanistic reasoning:**  
- Dual-site passivation eliminates both metal- and halide-related traps.  
- Aromatic dipole aligns levels, reducing interfacial carrier accumulation and suppression of nonradiative recombination.

**Classification:**  
- **Slow PL decay → low defect density, radiative recombination dominance.**

# Summary  
When introduced directly into the perovskite precursor as a small-molecule component of a co-SAM system, 4-ABnP participates in coordination-assisted crystallization and interfacial self-assembly, rather than post-deposition surface passivation. Through cooperative phosphonate–metal coordination, amine–halide stabilization, and aromatic dipole formation during film growth, 4-ABnP enables earlier and more synchronized nucleation, suppressed defect generation, and dipole-engineered buried-interface energetics. These effects manifest as earlier PL onset, slower PL decay, and ultimately translate into enhanced Voc, FF, stability, and overall PCE in co-SAM-engineered perovskite solar cells.
