## Antisolvent Volume
### Nucleation shock and perovskite network formation
Antisolvent volume directly controls **how aggressively you remove the host solvent (DMF/DMSO) and how rapidly you push the system into supersaturation**.
1. **Insufficient volume (e.g. 120 μL)**
- Only part of the wet film sees strong miscibility with the antisolvent; the solvent gradient across the thickness remains large.
- Nucleation is incomplete and localized, leaving residual solvent pockets that later collapse into pinholes or voids.
- Effective coverage may stay in the **30–70%** range; gray levels in many regions are **80–110** (light gray) with obvious “holes”.
2. **Optimal volume (e.g. around 150 μL)**
- Enough antisolvent to rapidly extract host solvent across the whole film thickness and area, but not so much that the film is mechanically damaged.
- You trigger a quasi-simultaneous nucleation front through the film, leading to a continuous percolated perovskite network.
- Coverage >**90–100%**, gray level **~57** or even down to **0–40** depending on thickness – a uniform dark film.
3. **Excessive volume (>optimal window)**
- The perovskite-rich phase may precipitate too violently; the upper part of the film can harden while the bottom still contains solvent.
- Later, differential contraction can cause wrinkles, delamination or voids. Microscopic wrinkling can correlate with performance losses when not controlled.
### Device metrics
- **Jsc**
- Insufficient volume → pinholes and under-crystallized regions → light passes to ETL without being absorbed, and carrier collection is incomplete; Jsc falls.
- Optimal volume → pinhole-free morphology and well-packed grains → maximal Jsc.
- Excessive volume → highly wrinkled, defect-rich films → optical scattering and recombination lower Jsc.
- **Voc & FF**
- Antisolvent treatment is one of the most powerful levers for eliminating pinholes and roughness, which is why “pinhole-free perovskite films” correlate with high Voc and FF in many reports.
- Insufficient or excessive volume both give either pinholes (low Rsh) or stress-induced cracks (high Rs and local shunts), depressing FF and Voc.
