# Compound: Para-Fluoro-Phenethylammonium Chloride  
*(p-F-PEACl; Molecular formula: C₈H₁₁ClFN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Para-fluoro-phenethylammonium chloride  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₁ClFN  
- **Ionic form:**  
  [p-F–C₆H₄–CH₂CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · Cl⁻
- **Structural features:**  
  The molecule consists of a phenethylammonium cation bearing a fluorine substituent at the para (4-) position of the phenyl ring, paired with a chloride counter-anion.  
  Para substitution confers higher molecular symmetry and reduced steric hindrance compared with meta substitution.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Para-Fluoro Substituent (4-F)
- **Electronic properties:**  
  - Strong σ-electron-withdrawing effect.  
  - Alters dipole orientation relative to meta-fluoro analogues, reducing overall dipole disorder.
- **Functional consequences:**  
  - Improves band alignment with the perovskite absorber.  
  - Lowers surface energy and suppresses non-radiative recombination.
- **Stability role:**  
  - Enhances hydrophobicity, strengthening the moisture barrier at the interface.

### 2.2 Phenyl Ring
- **π-Conjugated backbone:**  
  - Provides a rigid, coplanar aromatic surface due to para substitution.  
- **Interfacial interactions:**  
  - Enables π–π stacking and cation–π interactions with under-coordinated surface Pb²⁺ sites.  
  - Dense molecular packing promotes uniform interfacial coverage.

### 2.3 Ethylene Linker (–CH₂CH₂–)
- **Conformational flexibility:**  
  - Allows the –NH₃⁺ headgroup to dock into the perovskite cage.  
  - Positions the para-fluorophenyl segment outward to form a hydrophobic overlayer.

### 2.4 Protonated Ammonium with Chloride Counter-Ion (–NH₃⁺ · Cl⁻)
- **Defect passivation:**  
  - The ammonium group forms N–H···X hydrogen bonds with surface halide vacancies, reducing trap-state density.
- **Halide role:**  
  - Cl⁻ can migrate into the lattice or grain boundaries, improving crystallinity and phase stability.[1][2]

---

## 3. Interfacial and Phase-Engineering Mechanism

- Para-fluoro substitution reduces steric hindrance and promotes uniform spreading of the molecule at the perovskite surface.
- The –NH₃⁺ group and Cl⁻ counter-ion cooperatively interact with surface defects, enabling effective defect passivation and energy-level alignment.
- Bulky phenethylammonium cations convert only the outer unit cells of a 3D perovskite into nanometre-thin Ruddlesden–Popper slabs.
- This results in a conformal 2D/3D interface when the layer is thin and well oriented.[1][2]

---

## 4. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Reduced surface recombination due to effective passivation.  
- Improved energy-level alignment at the interface.  
- Enhanced moisture resistance from the hydrophobic para-fluorophenyl overlayer.  
- Potential performance enhancement when the quasi-2D layer is thin and uniformly oriented.
