# Compound: Phenethylammonium Bromide  
*(PEABr; Molecular formula: C₈H₁₂BrN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Phenethylammonium bromide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₂BrN  
- **Ionic composition:**  
  Phenethylammonium cation (PEA⁺) paired with bromide anion (Br⁻)
- **Structural features:**  
  Composed of a rigid hydrophobic phenyl ring connected via a two-carbon alkyl spacer to a terminal primary ammonium group (–NH₃⁺).  
  The combination of the aromatic ring, ammonium functionality, and bromide anion enables hydrogen bonding and coordination interactions, facilitating the formation of low-dimensional perovskite phases at surfaces.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Phenethylammonium Cation (PEA⁺)
- **Type:** Aryl-primary ammonium ion.
- **Charge distribution:**  
  Positive charge localized on the terminal –NH₃⁺ group.
- **Conformational properties:**  
  The phenyl–ethyl–ammonium architecture is rigid yet flexible; rotation of the phenyl plane and C₂ linker allows interfacial self-adaptation.
- **Hydrogen-bonding capability:**  
  N–H protons act as strong hydrogen-bond donors, forming N–H···X⁻ (X = Br⁻, I⁻, Cl⁻) networks.
- **Defect passivation:**  
  On perovskite surfaces, these hydrogen bonds interact with exposed halide vacancies or Pb–X-terminated facets, contributing to defect passivation.
- **Hydrophobic and stacking effects:**  
  The phenyl ring provides π–π stacking capability and a hydrophobic shield, suppressing moisture ingress and tuning the stacking orientation (n-value distribution) of low-dimensional perovskites.[1][2]


### 2.2 Bromide Anion (Br⁻)
- **Type:** Spherical halide anion.
- **Role in precursor solutions:**  
  Coordinates with Pb²⁺ to form intermediate complexes such as [PbBr₆]⁴⁻, modulating colloid size and crystallization kinetics.
- **Role in solid films:**  
  After annealing, Br⁻ can segregate at grain boundaries or surfaces, passivating bromide vacancies and suppressing non-radiative recombination.
- **Morphological influence:**  
  Controlled incorporation of Br⁻ improves film morphology and orientation order.[1][2]

---

## 3. Interfacial and Phase-Formation Mechanism

- PEA⁺ provides strong hydrogen-bonding interactions and hydrophobic surface coverage through its ammonium group and phenyl ring.
- Br⁻ participates in coordination with Pb²⁺ during precursor stages and in vacancy passivation after film formation.
- The combined cation–anion interactions facilitate the formation and stabilization of low-dimensional perovskite phases at surfaces and interfaces.[3]

---

## 4. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Reduced surface and grain-boundary defect density.  
- Improved crystallization control and orientation order.  
- Enhanced moisture resistance due to hydrophobic phenyl shielding.  
- Suppressed non-radiative recombination and improved overall film quality.
