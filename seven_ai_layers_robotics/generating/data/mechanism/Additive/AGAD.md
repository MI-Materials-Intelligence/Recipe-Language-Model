# Compound: (S)-2-Amino-3-guanidinopropionic Acid Dihydrochloride  
*(AGAD; Molecular formula: C₄H₁₁N₅O₂·2HCl)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** (S)-2-amino-3-guanidinopropionic acid dihydrochloride  
- **Molecular formula:** C₄H₁₁N₅O₂·2HCl  
- **Structural features:**  
  The molecule contains both a guanidinium group (–NH–C(=NH)–NH₂) and a carboxylic acid group (–COOH).  
- **Zwitterionic nature:**  
  The coexistence of cationic and anionic functional groups gives rise to zwitterionic character, enabling hydrogen bonding, ionic bonding, and coordination interactions with a wide range of ions and functional groups.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Properties

### 2.1 Guanidinium Moiety (–NH–C(=NH)–NH₂)
- **Type:**  
  Protonated guanidinium cation (–NH–C(=NH₂⁺)–NH₂) under physiological conditions.
- **Electronic and structural properties:**  
  - Delocalized positive charge over a planar, Y-shaped π-system.  
  - Extremely strong hydrogen-bond donor with three N–H donors.
- **Binding capabilities:**  
  - Forms salt bridges (ionic bonds) with anions.  
  - Acts as a multidentate ligand for metal coordination via imine and amine nitrogen atoms.

### 2.2 Carboxylic Acid Group (–COOH / –COO⁻)
- **Type:**  
  Exists as a carboxylate anion (–COO⁻) under physiological or basic conditions and as neutral –COOH at low pH.
- **Electronic and binding properties:**  
  - Provides a localized negative charge in the zwitterionic form.  
  - Strong hydrogen-bond acceptor through two equivalent oxygen atoms.  
  - Forms ionic bonds with cations and coordinates metal ions via bidentate chelation.  
  - Acts as a hydrogen-bond donor only in the protonated –COOH form.

### 2.3 Overall Zwitterionic Character
- The simultaneous presence of a guanidinium cation and a carboxylate anion creates an internal salt bridge.  
- This internal charge separation results in a high dipole moment, high water solubility, and versatile external binding modes, including hydrogen bonding, ionic interactions, and metal coordination.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation and Crystallization Mechanism

- **Metal-defect passivation:**  
  The guanidinium moiety coordinates with under-coordinated Pb²⁺ ions through Pb–N bond formation, effectively passivating deep-level Pb-related defects in the bulk perovskite.

- **A-site stabilization:**  
  The carboxyl group forms hydrogen bonds with A-site cations (e.g., FA⁺), suppressing vacancy formation and stabilizing the lattice.

- **Crystallization modulation:**  
  During the wet-film stage, AGAD slows nucleation kinetics, suppressing pinhole formation and reducing grain-boundary defects.[1]

---

## 4. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Promotes the formation of dense and uniform perovskite films with large crystal grains.  
- Enhances structural stability of the perovskite lattice.  
- Reduces trap-assisted (non-radiative) recombination.  
- Collectively contributes to improved optoelectronic quality and enhanced device performance.
