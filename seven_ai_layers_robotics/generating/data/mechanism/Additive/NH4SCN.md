# Compound: Ammonium Thiocyanate  
*(NH₄SCN; Molecular formula: NH₄SCN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Ammonium thiocyanate  
- **Molecular formula:** NH₄SCN (also written as CH₄N₂S)  
- **Ionic composition:**  
  NH₄⁺ (ammonium cation) and SCN⁻ (thiocyanate anion)
- **Structural characteristics:**  
  The compound consists of a tetrahedral ammonium cation and a linear thiocyanate ligand.  
  Both sulfur and nitrogen atoms in SCN⁻ are available for coordination or hydrogen-bond interactions.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

### 2.1 Ammonium Cation (NH₄⁺)
- **Hydrogen-bonding role:**  
  The N–H protons act as strong hydrogen-bond donors, forming N–H···X⁻ (X = I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻) hydrogen-bond networks with halide ions at perovskite surfaces or grain boundaries.
- **Electrostatic passivation:**  
  NH₄⁺ adsorbs onto negatively charged defect sites such as PbI₃⁻ or SnI₃⁻ through electrostatic interactions, reducing trap-state density.
- **Defect stabilization:**  
  Contributes to stabilization of surface halide vacancies and A-site-related defects.

### 2.2 Thiocyanate Anion (SCN⁻)
- **Bidentate coordination capability:**  
  The linear SCN⁻ ligand can coordinate to Pb²⁺ or Sn²⁺ through either the sulfur or nitrogen terminus.
- **Intermediate formation:**  
  Forms metal–thiocyanate complexes (e.g., [Pb(SCN)₆]⁴⁻) or bridging structures in precursor solutions, modulating coordination equilibria and colloidal size.
- **Orientation control:**  
  Coordination of SCN⁻ to Pb²⁺ induces preferential (101) crystal-plane orientation.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation and Crystallization Mechanism

- **Cationic defect passivation:**  
  NH₄⁺ forms hydrogen bonds with surface halide ions and electrostatically binds to negatively charged defects, passivating halide and A-site vacancies.
- **Metal-site coordination:**  
  SCN⁻ coordinates to under-coordinated Pb²⁺ via its S or N atom, forming Pb–SCN complexes that regulate nucleation behavior.
- **Crystallization modulation:**  
  These interactions collectively promote controlled nucleation and growth, leading to dense, large-grain perovskite films.[1]

---

## 4. Structural Orientation and Stability Effects

- Preferential crystal orientation enhances carrier mobility.  
- Reduced trap density suppresses non-radiative recombination.  
- SCN⁻ coordination stabilizes the crystal lattice against moisture ingress and ion migration.[2]

---

## 5. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Formation of dense perovskite films with enlarged grain size.  
- Lower defect density at surfaces and grain boundaries.  
- Improved structural stability and suppressed non-radiative recombination.  
- Overall enhancement of optoelectronic quality and device performance.[3]
