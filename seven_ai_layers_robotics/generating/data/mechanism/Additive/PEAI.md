# Compound: Phenylethylammonium Iodide  
*(PEAI; Molecular formula: C₈H₁₂IN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Phenylethylammonium iodide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₂IN  
- **Ionic composition:**  
  Phenethylammonium cation (PEA⁺) paired with iodide anion (I⁻)
- **Structural features:**  
  The PEA⁺ cation combines a hydrophobic phenyl ring with a terminal primary ammonium group (–NH₃⁺) linked by a flexible two-carbon alkyl spacer.  
  This architecture enables hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals contacts with the perovskite lattice, facilitating self-assembly into 2D or quasi-2D surface layers.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Phenethylammonium Cation (C₆H₅CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺)
- **Type:** Aryl-primary ammonium ion.
- **Charge and conformation:**  
  - A single positive charge localized on the terminal –NH₃⁺ group.  
  - The phenyl–ethylene–ammonium motif is semi-rigid and rotationally flexible, allowing adaptation to different interfacial environments.
- **Hydrogen-bonding capability:**  
  - N–H protons act as strong hydrogen-bond donors.  
  - Forms N–H···X⁻ (X = I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻) hydrogen-bond networks.  
  - On perovskite surfaces, interacts directly with Pb–I-terminated facets or iodide vacancies, contributing to surface-defect passivation.
- **Hydrophobic and stacking effects:**  
  - The aromatic phenyl ring enables π–π stacking.  
  - Provides a hydrophobic barrier that suppresses moisture ingress and helps control the n-value distribution in layered or quasi-2D perovskites.

### 2.2 Iodide Anion (I⁻)
- **Type:** Spherical halide anion.
- **Role in precursor solutions:**  
  - Coordinates with Pb²⁺ or Sn²⁺ to form intermediate complexes such as [PbI₆]⁴⁻, modulating colloid size and crystallization kinetics.
- **Role in solid films:**  
  - After deposition and annealing, iodide can segregate at grain boundaries or surfaces.  
  - Passivates iodide vacancies and suppresses non-radiative recombination.  
  - Controlled incorporation improves crystal orientation and film density.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation and Interfacial Mechanism

- The terminal –NH₃⁺ group of PEA⁺ serves as a strong hydrogen-bond donor and electrostatically anchors to halide-terminated perovskite surfaces.
- I⁻ supplements lattice iodide on Pb–I terminations, enabling like-ion passivation.
- On lead-iodide-rich facets, PEA⁺ forms ion pairs (–NH₃⁺···I⁻) and hydrogen bonds with surface halides.
- The –NH₃⁺ end binds to under-coordinated surface sites, while the bulky aromatic segment sterically and electronically caps the surface.[1][2]

---

## 4. Interfacial and Film-Level Effects

- Reduction of surface and grain-boundary defect density.  
- Lowered interfacial recombination velocity.  
- Formation and stabilization of 2D or quasi-2D surface layers.  
- Improved moisture resistance due to hydrophobic aromatic shielding.[2][3]

---

## 5. Resulting Device-Level Implications

- Suppressed non-radiative recombination at interfaces.  
- Enhanced film quality and structural stability.  
- Improved operational stability and photovoltaic performance.
