## Spin Coating Time PVK 1
###  Influence on wetting, solvent loss, and pre-crystallization
1. **Too short time**
- The solution does not fully wet or level before entering the high-speed stage. Some regions remain thick droplet-like, others thin.
- As a result, during PVK2 and antisolvent, the local supersaturation history is very different: some areas nucleate early, others late → broad grain-size distribution and local porosity.
2. **Optimal time**
- Enough time for complete coverage, partial solvent loss, and homogeneous concentration, but not long enough to allow significant spontaneous nucleation.
- The resulting wet film is uniform in thickness and composition, and the subsequent steps define final morphology much more predictably.
3. **Too long time**
- Excess evaporation at the slow stage increases solute concentration and viscosity before PVK2 begins.
- Pre-crystallization may occur in patches (especially at edges), creating nuclei that resist being leveled in the fast step and act as “anchors” for cracks and holes later.
### Impact on device metrics
- Too short or too long spin time 1 mainly **amplifies inhomogeneity**, which:
- Increases local trap density and interface recombination → lower Voc.
- Produces paths with higher Rs and local shunts → lower FF.
With the **optimal slow-step duration**, you remove these large-scale nonuniformities, so the “fine tuning” of PVK2, antisolvent and annealing can manifest as smooth changes in Jsc, Voc and FF instead of catastrophic failures.
