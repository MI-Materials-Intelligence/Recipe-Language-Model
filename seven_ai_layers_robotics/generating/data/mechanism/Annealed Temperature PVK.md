## Annealed Temperature PVK
### Phase evolution, grain growth and stability
Thermal annealing drives:
- Solvent removal and adduct decomposition,
- Phase transformation (e.g. δ→α-phase in FA-rich systems),
- Grain growth and defect annealing.
1. **Too low temperature (<100 °C)**
- Adducts do not fully decompose; residual solvent and intermediate phases (e.g. δ-FAPbI₃) remain.
- Grain size is small, and the film is often porous with many intragranular voids.
- Top-view images: mean gray level >**60**, with visible speckles and texture.
2. **Optimal temperature (~100–110 °C)**
- Enough thermal energy to complete adduct breakdown and transform δ-phase to the desired black α-phase.
- Grain growth is promoted without excessive decomposition; lateral grains coalesce, reducing grain-boundary density.
- Morphology: compact, dark film with gray level ~**13–40**, high coverage, and relatively low roughness.
3. **Too high temperature (e.g. 120 °C or above for sensitive compositions)**
- Additional thermal stress can cause partial decomposition (PbI₂ segregation) and phase segregation in mixed-halide or mixed-cation perovskites.
- These phases appear as brighter or differently textured regions (gray **66–76** and heterogeneous contrast), often at grain boundaries or near interfaces.
### Device metrics
- **Jsc**
- Low temperature → residual δ-phase and voids lower the effective absorption and shorten carrier diffusion length; Jsc is limited.
- Optimal temperature → better crystallinity and improved transport give higher Jsc.
- Excessive temperature → decomposition and rough interfaces can again restrict Jsc.
- **Voc**
- Voc is highly sensitive to nonradiative recombination:
- Low T → many defects and traps → low Voc.
- Optimal T → fewer traps and better phase purity → Voc maximized.
- Too high T → new defects (PbI₂ islands, vacancy clusters) form, pulling Voc down again.
- **FF**
- Films annealed too cold have poor connectivity and high Rs, hurting FF.
- Over-annealed films can develop micro-cracks or rough interfaces, reducing Rsh and increasing Rs simultaneously, which degrades FF.
- **PCE**
- There is a clear “annealing window” around 100 °C, outside of which PCE deteriorates even if only grain size or only phase purity improves; the balance of all effects is what matters.
