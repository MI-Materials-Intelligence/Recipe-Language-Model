# Compound: Lead Chloride  
*(PbCl₂; Molecular formula: PbCl₂)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Lead chloride  
- **Molecular formula:** PbCl₂  
- **Structural features:**  
  Composed of layered PbCl₆ octahedral motifs that can assemble into corner-sharing chains or layered frameworks.  
  Readily forms coordination complexes or double salts with halides and organic cations, providing a controllable chloride source for perovskite systems.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Pb²⁺ Center
- **Type:** Soft Lewis-acid metal site.
- **Coordination properties:**  
  - Readily coordinates with halides, carboxylates, amines, thioethers, and other Lewis bases.  
  - Forms complexes such as [PbClₓ]^(2–x).
- **Role in precursors:**  
  - Acts as a Pb²⁺ source in perovskite precursor solutions.  
  - Forms soluble adducts with solvents or additives, controlling colloid size and crystallization kinetics.

### 2.2 Chloride Ligand (Cl⁻)
- **Type:** Spherical halide anion and hard Lewis base.
- **Structural roles:**  
  - Combines with Pb²⁺ to form octahedral [PbCl₆]⁴⁻ units, corner-sharing [PbCl₃]⁻ₙ chains, or 2D layered structures.  
  - Determines the dimensionality (1D, 2D, or 3D) of the resulting inorganic framework.
- **Interfacial interactions:**  
  - Acts as a hydrogen-bond acceptor, forming N–H···Cl⁻ networks with organic ammonium cations (e.g., MA⁺, FA⁺, PEA⁺).  
  - Promotes precursor dispersion and passivates surface or grain-boundary defects.

---

## 3. Crystallization and Defect-Modulation Mechanism

- **Lewis-acid/base synergy:**  
  Pb²⁺ coordinates with halides, carboxylates, and amines to form soluble precursor complexes that regulate colloidal species and crystallization behavior.[1]
- **Chloride-mediated effects:**
  - Stabilizes organic cations through N–H···Cl⁻ hydrogen-bond networks.  
  - Passivates grain-boundary defects and enhances the ability to accelerate the direct formation of α-FAPbI3.[2][3]

---

## 4. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Enlarged perovskite grain size.  
- Reduced grain-boundary defect density.  
- Extended carrier diffusion length.  
- Enhanced open-circuit voltage (Voc) and overall device performance.
