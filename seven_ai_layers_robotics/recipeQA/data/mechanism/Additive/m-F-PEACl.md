# Compound: Meta-Fluoro-Phenethylammonium Chloride  
*(m-F-PEACl; Molecular formula: C₈H₁₁ClFN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Meta-fluoro-phenethylammonium chloride  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₁ClFN  
- **Structural features:**  
  - Fluorine substituent at the meta (3-) position of the phenyl ring.  
  - Smallest molecular volume among the phenethylammonium halide series.  
  - Composed of a phenethylammonium cation paired with a chloride anion.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Meta-Fluoro Substituent (m-F)
- **Electronic properties:**  
  - Fluorine exhibits the highest electronegativity, creating a strong induced dipole.  
  - Strong σ-electron-withdrawing effect lowers the HOMO level of the cation.
- **Functional consequences:**  
  - Improves energy-level alignment with the perovskite valence band.  
  - Assists charge separation through local dipole formation.  
  - Enhances hydrophobicity, contributing to improved moisture stability.
- **Structural impact:**  
  - No heavy-atom effect and minimal mass, resulting in the least lattice disturbance among halogen-substituted analogues.

### 2.2 Phenyl Ring
- **Role:**  
  - Provides π–π stacking and van der Waals interactions with the inorganic framework.  
  - Passivates under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites via cation–π interactions.

### 2.3 Ethylene Linker (–CH₂CH₂–)
- **Function:**  
  - Supplies conformational flexibility.  
  - Allows the –NH₃⁺ headgroup to dock into the perovskite cage while the fluoro-aryl segment forms a hydrophobic overlayer.

### 2.4 Protonated Ammonium with Chloride Counter-Ion (–NH₃⁺ · Cl⁻)
- **Hydrogen-bond network:**  
  - The protonated amine forms hydrogen bonds with surface halide vacancies, reducing trap-state density.
- **Halide role:**  
  - Cl⁻ can migrate to heal iodide vacancies, suppressing non-radiative recombination.  
  - Influences early-stage crystallization and typically departs during perovskite conversion.

---

## 3. Dual-Halide Passivation Mechanism

- m-F-PEACl acts as a highly polar, dual-halide passivation agent.  
- **Halide-vacancy and cation-defect passivation:**  
  - F⁻ selectively fills halide vacancies.  
  - Cl⁻ neutralizes Pb²⁺ dangling bonds, collectively reducing trap density.
- **Moisture resistance:**  
  - The C–F bond contributes to enhanced resistance against moisture ingress.

---

## 4. Interface and Phase Engineering Effects

- Meta-fluoro substitution tunes molecular packing and orientation.  
- Enables formation of a thin, well-ordered Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) overlayer.  
- The ordered overlayer passivates traps, suppresses ion migration, and provides gentle band-edge and work-function adjustment.

---

## 5. Resulting Film and Device-Level Implications

- Reduced defect density and suppressed non-radiative recombination.  
- Improved interfacial stability and moisture tolerance.  
- Enhanced charge separation and energy-level alignment at the perovskite interface.
