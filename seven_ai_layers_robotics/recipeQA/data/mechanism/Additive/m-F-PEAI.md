# Compound: Meta-Fluoro-Phenylethylammonium Iodide  
*(m-F-PEAI; Molecular formula: C₈H₁₁FIN)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Meta-fluoro-phenylethylammonium iodide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₁FIN  
- **Ionic form:**  
  [m-F–C₆H₄–CH₂CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · I⁻
- **Structural features:**  
  The molecule consists of a phenyl ring substituted at the meta (3-) position with a fluorine atom, connected via an ethyl linker to a terminal primary ammonium group (–CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺), forming an ion pair with iodide.  
  The meta substitution orients the molecular dipole approximately perpendicular to the phenyl ring, matching the vertical dipole direction upon adsorption on a perovskite surface.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Fluorine Substituent (–F)
- **Electronic properties:**  
  - Highest electronegativity (χ = 3.98), generating a strong molecular dipole (~2.0 D).  
  - Enables downward tuning of the surface work function and improved energy-level alignment.
- **Structural and thermal properties:**  
  - Short C–F bond length (~1.35 Å) with high vibrational frequency.  
  - Confers excellent thermal stability without introducing heavy-atom effects.

### 2.2 Primary Ammonium Cation (–CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺)
- **Defect passivation:**  
  - Forms N–H···X hydrogen bonds or N–H···Pb coordination with under-coordinated Pb²⁺/Sn²⁺ at surfaces and grain boundaries.  
  - Effectively passivates cation-related defect sites.

### 2.3 Iodide Anion (I⁻)
- **Like-ion passivation:**  
  - Identical to lattice iodide, enabling reduction of iodine-vacancy concentration.  
- **Dimensionality control:**  
  - Participates in the formation of Ruddlesden–Popper quasi-2D phases for phase and dimensionality engineering.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation and Interfacial Mechanism

- The –CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺ group provides strong hydrogen bonding and N–H···Pb interactions to passivate under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites.  
- I⁻ supplies like-ion passivation to heal iodine vacancies.  
- The meta-fluoro substituent introduces a vertical molecular dipole aligned with the perovskite surface field, reducing surface dipole disorder and downshifting the work function.[1]

---

## 4. Dimensionality and Crystallization Effects

- During annealing, m-F-PEAI forms a thin quasi-2D Ruddlesden–Popper layer (n = 2–4).  
- Promotes vertical grain orientation and enlarges crystal domains.  
- Acts as a barrier against moisture infiltration.[2]

---

## 5. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Suppresses non-radiative recombination through effective defect passivation.  
- Enhances energy-level alignment and reduces surface recombination losses.  
- Reduces ion migration and hysteresis.  
- Leads to improved open-circuit voltage (Voc), enhanced operational stability, and overall device performance.[3]
