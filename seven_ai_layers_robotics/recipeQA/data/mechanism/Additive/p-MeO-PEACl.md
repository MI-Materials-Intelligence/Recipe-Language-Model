# Compound: Para-Methoxy-Phenethylammonium Chloride  
*(p-MeO-PEACl; Molecular formula: C₉H₁₄ClNO)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Para-methoxy-phenethylammonium chloride  
- **Molecular formula:** C₉H₁₄ClNO  
- **Ionic composition:**  
  Para-methoxy-phenethylammonium cation paired with chloride anion (Cl⁻)
- **Structural features:**  
  The molecule consists of a phenethylammonium scaffold bearing a methoxy group (–OCH₃) at the para (4-) position of the phenyl ring.  
  Para substitution imparts a symmetric molecular geometry, favoring ordered interfacial assembly.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Characteristics

### 2.1 Para-Methoxy Substituent (4-MeO)
- **Electronic properties:**  
  - Strong electron-donating (+M) effect increases π-electron density of the phenyl ring.
- **Functional roles:**  
  - Enhances cation–π interactions with under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites.  
  - Lone-pair electrons on the oxygen atom provide additional hydrogen-bond-acceptor sites.  
  - Can anchor surface water molecules or interact with halide vacancies.
- **Stability contribution:**  
  - The hydrophobic methoxy group improves moisture resistance of the interfacial layer.

### 2.2 Phenyl Ring
- **π-Conjugated backbone:**  
  - Provides a rigid, coplanar aromatic plane due to para substitution.  
- **Interfacial interactions:**  
  - Enables π–π stacking and cation–π passivation of electron-deficient surface Pb²⁺ atoms.  
  - Dense molecular packing supports uniform surface coverage.

### 2.3 Ethylene Linker (–CH₂CH₂–)
- **Conformational flexibility:**  
  - Allows the –NH₃⁺ headgroup to insert into the perovskite cage.  
  - Positions the para-methoxy phenyl segment outward to form an electron-rich, hydrophobic overlayer.

### 2.4 Protonated Ammonium with Chloride Counter-Ion (–NH₃⁺ · Cl⁻)
- **Defect passivation:**  
  - The ammonium group forms N–H···X hydrogen bonds with halide vacancies, reducing trap-state density.
- **Halide role:**  
  - Cl⁻ can migrate to fill iodide or bromide vacancies, improving crystallinity and phase stability.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation and Interfacial Mechanism

- Electron donation from the para-methoxy group strengthens cation–π interactions with under-coordinated Pb²⁺.  
- Hydrogen bonding from the –NH₃⁺ group and ether oxygen stabilizes surface halide vacancies and polar sites.  
- Chloride ions contribute to halide-vacancy healing and intermediate-phase regulation.[1]

---

## 4. Crystallization and Phase Behavior

- p-MeO-PEACl delays perovskite crystallization kinetics.  
- Promotes optimized crystal orientation and more ordered two-dimensional capping layers.  
- Improves overall film morphology without altering the intrinsic band gap.[1]

---

## 5. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Reduced surface and grain-boundary defect density.  
- Enhanced moisture resistance and interfacial stability.  
- Improved crystallinity and phase stability.  
- Overall enhancement in perovskite film quality, device performance, and operational stability.
