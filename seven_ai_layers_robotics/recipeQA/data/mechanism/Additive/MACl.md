# Compound: Methylammonium Chloride  
*(MACl; Molecular formula: CH₃NH₃Cl)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **Full name:** Methylammonium chloride  
- **Molecular formula:** CH₃NH₃Cl  
- **Ionic composition:**  
  CH₃NH₃⁺ (methylammonium cation) and Cl⁻ (chloride anion)
- **Physical characteristics:**  
  White, hygroscopic crystalline solid at room temperature; readily soluble in water, DMF, DMSO, and other polar solvents.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

### 2.1 Methylammonium Cation (CH₃NH₃⁺)(m-Br-PEACl.md)
- **Type:** Primary ammonium ion (–NH₃⁺).
- **Structural properties:**  
  - Bears a single positive charge.  
  - Adopts a tetrahedral–pyramidal geometry that is rotationally flexible but constrained by the crystal lattice.
- **Interaction characteristics:**  
  - N–H protons act as strong hydrogen-bond donors.  
  - Forms N–H···Cl⁻ hydrogen bonds with chloride and other anions.  
  - Does not directly coordinate to Pb²⁺ or Sn²⁺; instead, influences the perovskite octahedral framework indirectly through hydrogen-bonding interactions.

### 2.2 Chloride Anion (Cl⁻)
- **Type:** Spherical halide anion.
- **Interaction characteristics:**  
  - Bears a single negative charge.  
  - Acts as a strong hydrogen-bond acceptor, forming ion pairs and extended three-dimensional hydrogen-bond networks with –NH₃⁺ groups.
- **Role in perovskite systems:**  
  - In precursor solutions, can coordinate to Pb²⁺ or Sn²⁺ to form intermediate complexes such as [PbCl₆]⁴⁻.  
  - In thin films, may be partially incorporated into the lattice or enriched at grain boundaries, influencing morphology and phase behavior.

---

## 3. Crystallization and Defect-Related Mechanism

- **Hydrogen-bond-mediated crystallization control:**  
  CH₃NH₃⁺ stabilizes the perovskite octahedral framework and regulates crystallization kinetics via an N–H···Cl⁻ hydrogen-bond network.

- **Intermediate formation and defect modulation:**  
  Cl⁻ coordinates with Pb²⁺ to form intermediate complexes that delay crystallization and assist in passivating grain-boundary defects.

- **Lattice and grain-boundary effects:**  
  Chloride ions can be partially doped into the lattice or accumulate at grain boundaries, synergistically improving film density, crystal orientation, and phase stability.[1]

---

## 4. Resulting Film and Device-Level Effects

- Produces denser and more uniformly oriented perovskite films.  
- Enhances phase stability and reduces defect density, particularly at grain boundaries.  
- Collectively contributes to significantly improved perovskite film quality and device efficiency.[2]
