# Compound: Piperazine Iodide  
*(Molecular formula: C₄H₁₁IN₂)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **English name:** Piperazine iodide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₄H₁₁IN₂  
- **Ionic form:**  
  [C₄H₁₁N₂]⁺ · I⁻  
- **Core scaffold:**  
  Piperazine ring adopting a chair conformation, containing two secondary amine sites with asymmetric protonation (one protonated, one neutral).

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

### 2.1 Protonated Secondary Ammonium (–NH₂⁺–)
- **Electrostatic anchoring:**  
  The protonated –NH₂⁺– group passivates negatively charged defects, such as iodide vacancies, through electrostatic attraction.
- **Defect compensation role:**  
  Acts as the primary ionic interaction site between the molecule and the perovskite surface.

### 2.2 Neutral Secondary Amine (–NH–)
- **Hydrogen-bond donation:**  
  Functions as a strong hydrogen-bond donor, forming N–H···X hydrogen bonds with halide ions (I⁻) or atoms within the perovskite lattice.
- **Cooperative passivation:**  
  Works synergistically with the protonated ammonium group to enable hydrogen-bond-based defect passivation.

### 2.3 Intramolecular Interaction
- **Intramolecular charge transfer:**  
  The lone pair on the neutral amine can stabilize the adjacent protonated ammonium group through intramolecular interaction, modulating local electron density.

### 2.4 Conformational Adaptability
- **Ring flexibility:**  
  The piperazine ring in a chair conformation provides adaptable geometry, allowing effective binding to a variety of defect sites on the perovskite surface.

---

## 3. Role of Iodide Ion (I⁻)

- **Halide vacancy compensation:**  
  Supplies iodide ions to fill iodide vacancies, reducing deep-level trap states.
- **Hydrogen-bond network node:**  
  Acts as a hydrogen-bond acceptor, forming a network with the molecule’s –NH– and –NH₂⁺– groups and the surrounding lattice[1].
- **Interfacial stabilization:**  
  Participation in the hydrogen-bond network contributes to stabilization of the perovskite interface structure[2].

---

## 4. Multi-Site Defect Passivation Mechanism

- The passivation mechanism follows a **single-site electrostatic anchoring + single-site hydrogen-bond passivation** model.
- **Electrostatic component:**  
  The –NH₂⁺– group attracts and stabilizes iodide vacancies.
- **Hydrogen-bond component:**  
  The neutral –NH– group forms hydrogen bonds with under-coordinated iodide ions or with the coordination environment of lead ions.
- **Synergistic effect:**  
  The combined interactions effectively reduce interfacial trap density.

---

## 5. Ion-Migration Suppression

- Binding of the molecule to the perovskite surface through an extended hydrogen-bond network creates a partial physical barrier to ion migration.
- The associated iodide ion is incorporated into this network and exhibits low mobility, contributing to suppressed ion migration at the interface.
