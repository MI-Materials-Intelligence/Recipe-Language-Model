# Compound: Ethylenediammonium Diiodide  
*(EDADI; Molecular formula: C₂H₁₀N₂·2I⁻)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **English name:** Ethylenediammonium diiodide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₂H₁₀N₂·2I⁻  
- **Ionic representation:** [H₃N⁺–CH₂–CH₂–NH₃⁺]·2I⁻  
- **Core scaffold:**  
  Doubly protonated ethylenediamine forming a symmetric dication (enH₂²⁺).
- **Ionic composition:**  
  - Cation: ethylenediammonium (enH₂²⁺)  
  - Anions: two iodide ions (I⁻)
- **Linear SMILES:**  
  `[NH3+]C-C[NH3+].[I-].[I-]`
- **2D structural description:**  
  Linear N⁺–C–C–N⁺ chain, with each ammonium end electrostatically paired to an I⁻ counter-ion.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

- **Two primary ammonium groups (–NH₃⁺):**  
  Provide electrostatic compensation and hydrogen-bond interactions with surface iodide vacancies and under-coordinated Pb²⁺ sites, reducing defect density.

- **Ethylene linker (–CH₂–CH₂–):**  
  Acts as a short and flexible spacer that positions the two ammonium groups at an optimal distance from the perovskite surface, avoiding lattice distortion while maintaining film compactness.

- **Overall structural functionality:**  
  As a diammonium salt with an inter-ammonium distance shorter than 4 Å, EDADI can bridge adjacent metal–halide octahedra without introducing a hydrophobic gap, combining structural stability with preserved charge-transport pathways.

---
## 3. Defect Passivation and Structural Roles

### 3.1 Surface-Defect Passivation
- Dual –NH₃⁺ groups compensate iodide vacancies through combined electrostatic attraction and N–H···I hydrogen bonding, lowering surface defect density.
- Released I⁻ anions directly fill halide vacancies, suppressing non-radiative recombination[1].

### 3.2 Dion–Jacobson-Type Quasi-2D Surface Formation
- EDADI can form a Dion–Jacobson-type quasi-2D surface “skin”.
- Bidentate N–H···I and N–H···Pb interactions bind deep trap states.
- Adjacent octahedra are cross-linked, enhancing resistance to lattice degradation.

### 3.3 Suppression of Sn²⁺ Oxidation
- EDADI coordinates to Sn–Pb perovskite surfaces.
- This interaction decreases the Sn⁴⁺ fraction, improving chemical stability in Sn-containing perovskites.

---

## 4. Film Morphology and Crystallinity Control

- Post-treatment with EDADI yields smoother perovskite surfaces.
- Promotes larger grain sizes and enhanced crystallinity.
- Maintains compact film morphology without introducing insulating gaps.

---

## 5. Photovoltaic Performance Impact

- Increases photoluminescence intensity and carrier lifetime.
- Reduces trap density at the surface and grain boundaries.
- Enables simultaneous enhancement of open-circuit voltage (Voc) and fill factor (FF).

---

## 6. Ion Migration Considerations and Synergistic Strategy

- **Potential ion-migration risk:**  
  Excess iodide ions may induce hysteresis if not controlled.

- **Synergistic mitigation with hydrazine monohydrochloride (HM):**  
  - HM forms N₂H₅⁺···I⁻ hydrogen bonds that immobilize mobile iodide ions.  
  - Cl⁻ from HM substitutes into iodide vacancies, further suppressing ion migration.  
  - HM donates electrons to Sn²⁺, suppressing oxidation and complementing EDADI’s surface passivation[2].

---

## 7. Design Implications

- Diammonium cations shorter than 4 Å, such as EDADI, uniquely unite the high stability of 2D Dion–Jacobson perovskites with the high carrier mobility of 3D phases.
- Future directions include fluorinated or π-conjugated diammonium analogues to deepen the valence band and enhance moisture resistance, as well as mixed-halide (Br/I) EDADI derivatives to tune lattice strain and suppress phase segregation.
