Compound: m-Br-PEACl (3-bromophenethylammonium chloride; C₈H₁₁BrClN)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [3-Br–C₆H₄–CH₂–CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · Cl⁻  
- Functional groups:  
  - **–NH₃⁺ (protonated primary amine):** forms electrostatic and hydrogen-bond interactions (N–H···X, X = I⁻/Cl⁻) with under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies, effectively passivating deep-level traps.  
  - **Meta-bromine (–Br):** renders the phenyl ring electron-deficient, enhancing π–π stacking and enabling weak Br···I halogen bonds that “lock” surface iodide ions and suppress ion migration.  
  - **Phenyl–ethyl chain:** hydrophobic aromatic–alkyl segment acts as a moisture barrier and provides mechanical flexibility for thermal-stress buffering.  
  - **Cl⁻ (chloride):** transiently compensates halide vacancies and forms N–H···Cl hydrogen-bond networks with –NH₃⁺; mostly volatilizes or remains at trace levels post-annealing.  
- Structural behavior: m-Br-PEACl forms a compact **2D/3D hybrid interface** via in-situ growth of a (m-Br-PEA)₂PbI₄-like layer. The electron-deficient aromatic structure drives strong molecular packing, resulting in dense, hydrophobic surface passivation that suppresses ion migration and enhances stability.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Dual-site defect passivation:**  
  The –NH₃⁺ headgroup electrostatically anchors to under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies through N–H···I hydrogen bonding, while Cl⁻ compensates halide deficiencies and neutralizes trap states. This combination effectively reduces deep-level traps and nonradiative recombination[1].  
- **m-Br-induced 2D/3D interface engineering:**  
  The meta-bromine substitution enhances π–π stacking and molecular ordering, promoting the formation of a thin, uniform 2D overlayer on the 3D perovskite. This 2D/3D graded interface smooths band alignment, suppresses interfacial recombination, and improves charge extraction, leading to higher Voc and FF[2].  
- **Ion-migration suppression:**  
  The Br···I halogen bonds and N–H···Cl hydrogen-bond networks immobilize mobile halide ions and stabilize Pb–I bonds, effectively reducing ion drift and J–V hysteresis.  
- **Crystallization and morphology optimization:**  
  m-Br-PEACl moderates crystal nucleation, yielding large-grain, dense perovskite films with fewer pinholes and smoother morphology. The improved film uniformity enhances carrier transport and reduces interfacial losses.  
- **Hydrophobic protection:**  
  The phenyl–alkyl backbone introduces a hydrophobic outer shell, minimizing moisture and oxygen ingress and improving device durability under ambient and thermal stress.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** increased due to reduced nonradiative recombination and improved band alignment.  
- **FF:** enhanced by better interfacial transport and suppressed ion migration.  
- **Jsc:** largely preserved, with occasional small gains from improved film uniformity.  
- **Stability:** significantly improved resistance to humidity, heat, and illumination due to the dense, hydrophobic, and ion-pinning surface layer.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Emergence of low-angle reflections (~4–6° 2θ)** indicating the formation of a 2D (m-Br-PEA)₂PbI₄-like surface phase.  
- **Main 3D perovskite peaks** retained with **FWHM narrowing**, reflecting enhanced crystallinity and reduced lattice strain.  
- **PbI₂ (≈12.6°)** peak intensity **reduced**, consistent with defect passivation and consumption of excess PbI₂.  
- Absence of impurity phases confirms coherent 2D/3D heterojunction formation and stable interfacial structure.
