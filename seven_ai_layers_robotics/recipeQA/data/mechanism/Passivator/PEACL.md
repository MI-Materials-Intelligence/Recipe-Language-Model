Compound: PEACl (Phenethylammonium chloride; C₈H₁₂ClN)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [C₆H₅–CH₂–CH₂–NH₃]⁺ · Cl⁻  
- Functional groups:  
  - **–NH₃⁺ (protonated primary amine):** forms ionic and hydrogen-bond interactions (N–H···I/Pb) with surface Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies, providing efficient defect passivation.  
  - **Phenethyl (C₆H₅–CH₂–CH₂–):** hydrophobic moiety forming a barrier that prevents H₂O/O₂ penetration, enhancing environmental stability.  
  - **Cl⁻ (chloride):** acts as a transient additive promoting crystallization and PEA⁺ incorporation; largely removed or redistributed during annealing, leaving trace residues that may reside at grain boundaries.  
- Structural behavior: PEACl can form ultrathin 2D phases such as 
-  on the perovskite surface, resulting in a 2D/3D heterostructure with improved interfacial energy alignment and defect suppression.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Crystallization control and film regulation:**  
  PEACl moderates perovskite crystal growth kinetics, stabilizing intermediate adducts and promoting larger, more uniform grains. This reduces grain-boundary recombination and enhances film smoothness[1].  
- **Surface and grain-boundary passivation:**  
  PEACl effectively passivates under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies through electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions. The small and mobile Cl⁻ ions can transiently fill defects or reside at GBs, further reducing trap density and suppressing nonradiative recombination[2].  
- **2D/3D interface construction:**  
  On the perovskite surface, PEACl treatment leads to the formation of a thin (PEA)₂Pb(Cl_{x}I_{1-x})₄-like 2D layer, producing a 2D/3D heterojunction. This layer functions as an electron-blocking barrier, mitigating interfacial recombination and improving energy-level alignment with charge-transport layers[3].  
  In p–i–n devices, dual incorporation (as additive and surface treatment) yields simultaneous passivation of grain boundaries and interfacial defects, enhancing charge extraction efficiency.  
- **Stability enhancement:**  
  The PEA⁺ layer and quasi-2D interfacial shell impart hydrophobic protection, preventing environmental degradation under humidity, heat, or light exposure. Additionally, the organic cation mitigates ion migration, preserving phase integrity and operational lifetime.

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** increased via reduced surface trap-assisted recombination and improved energy-level alignment.  
- **FF:** enhanced through better film morphology and lower interfacial series resistance.  
- **Jsc:** typically unchanged or slightly improved due to improved carrier collection efficiency.  
- **Stability:** substantially improved moisture, thermal, and illumination endurance due to hydrophobic surface protection and suppressed ion migration.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Main perovskite peaks** retain their positions, with **narrower FWHM** of (100)/(110) reflections indicating improved crystallinity and grain uniformity.  
- **PbI₂ (≈12.6°)** peak intensity generally **reduced**, reflecting suppressed residual PbI₂ and better surface passivation.  
- **Low-angle peaks (≈4–6° 2θ)** may appear, corresponding to thin (PEA)₂Pb(Cl_{x}I_{1-x})₄ or mixed halide 2D phases on the surface.  
- No additional impurity phases detected after optimal PEACl treatment, confirming successful formation of coherent 2D/3D interfaces.
