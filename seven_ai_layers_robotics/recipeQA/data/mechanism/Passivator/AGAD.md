# Compound: (S)-2-Amino-3-guanidinopropionic Acid Dihydrochloride  
*(AGAD; Molecular formula: C₄H₁₁ClN₄O₂)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **English name:** (S)-2-amino-3-guanidinopropionic acid dihydrochloride  
- **Molecular formula:** C₄H₁₁ClN₄O₂  
- **Salt form:** dihydrochloride salt, exhibiting high water solubility and suitability for solution processing.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

- **α-Amino group (–NH₂):**  
  Capable of forming hydrogen bonds or coordinating with surface defects.

- **Guanidino group (–NH–C(=NH)–NH₂):**  
  Strongly basic functional group providing multiple hydrogen-bond donors, enabling effective cation-vacancy passivation.

- **Carboxyl group (–COOH):**  
  Forms Pb–O coordination bonds, contributing to passivation of metal-related defects.

- **Overall structural functionality:**  
  This molecular architecture delivers a dual passivation mechanism combining carboxyl–metal coordination with guanidino-based hydrogen bonding.

---

## 3. Related Chemical Properties

### 3.1 Amphoteric Behavior
- The carboxyl group (pKₐ ≈ 2.1) and guanidino group (pKₐ ≈ 12.5) enable the molecule to act as both a proton donor and acceptor over a wide pH range.

### 3.2 Coordination and Chelation
- Carboxyl oxygen atoms and guanidino nitrogen atoms can bind metal ions such as Pb²⁺, Sn²⁺, and Cu²⁺.
- These interactions form stable five- or six-membered chelate rings, effectively passivating metal-related defects.

### 3.3 Hydrogen-Bond Network Formation
- Guanidino and α-amino groups supply N–H donors, while the carboxyl group provides C=O acceptors.
- This configuration readily forms hydrogen bonds with organic cations (FA⁺, MA⁺) or halide vacancies, lowering defect density.

### 3.4 Redox Inertness
- The compound remains chemically intact under perovskite processing temperatures (≤150 °C) and illumination, supporting long-term stability.

---

## 4. Defect Passivation Behavior

### 4.1 Bulk-Defect Passivation
- **Metal-related defects:**  
  Deprotonated carboxyl groups (–COO⁻) coordinate with under-coordinated Pb²⁺, passivating Pb vacancies and Pb clusters.
- **A-site cation vacancies:**  
  Guanidino and ammonium functionalities form hydrogen bonds or provide electrostatic compensation with FA⁺/MA⁺ vacancies, reducing A-site defects.
- **Halide vacancies:**  
  Cl⁻ ions fill halide vacancies and suppress ion migration[1].

---

## 5. Crystallization Control

- The presence of AGAD slows perovskite crystallization.
- This effect promotes the formation of dense, large-grained films with fewer pinholes and reduced grain-boundary defect density[1].

---

## 6. Synergistic Passivation Strategy (AGAD + AESM)

- AGAD and AESM self-assemble through hydrogen bonding interactions.
- This self-assembly distributes AESM uniformly at grain boundaries.
- The combined system repairs multiple surface and grain-boundary defects, dramatically suppressing non-radiative recombination[1].
