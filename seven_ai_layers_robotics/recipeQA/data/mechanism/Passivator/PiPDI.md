# Compound: Piperazinium Diiodide  
*(PiPDI; Molecular formula: C₄H₁₂I₂N₂)*

---

## 1. Chemical Structure

- **English name:** Piperazinium diiodide  
- **Molecular formula:** C₄H₁₂I₂N₂  
- **Ionic form:**  
  [C₄H₁₂N₂]²⁺ · 2 I⁻  
- **Core scaffold:**  
  Six-membered saturated piperazine ring (–CH₂–CH₂–NH–CH₂–CH₂–NH–), adopting a rigid chair conformation.

---

## 2. Functional Groups and Structural Features

### 2.1 Protonated Secondary Amine (–NH₂⁺–)
- **Dual-site anchoring:**  
  Two –NH₂⁺ groups on the piperazine ring simultaneously form ionic bonds and N–H···I hydrogen bonds with adjacent Pb²⁺ sites or halide vacancies, enabling dual-site defect passivation and lowering trap density.
- **Rigid cyclic structure:**  
  The chair conformation of the six-membered ring provides a steric barrier, creating a dense passivation layer at grain boundaries that blocks moisture and oxygen.
- **Dipole modulation:**  
  The intramolecular arrangement of the two ammonium groups generates a local dipole that fine-tunes the surface work function, boosting Voc and FF.

### 2.2 Iodide Ions (I⁻)
- **Halide compensation:**  
  Directly fill iodide vacancies, eliminating deep-level defects.
- **Ion pinning:**  
  Form N–H···I hydrogen-bond networks with –NH₂⁺ groups, suppressing Pb²⁺ and I⁻ migration and reducing hysteresis.
- **Crystallization aid:**  
  Trace diffusion of I⁻ during annealing promotes grain growth and reduces grain-boundary density.

---

## 3. Defect Passivation Behavior

- After coating the perovskite crystal surface, PiPDI effectively passivates non-radiative recombination centers, including halide vacancies and under-coordinated Pb²⁺ defects[1].
- The combined ionic bonding and hydrogen-bond interactions reduce defect-state density[2].

---

## 4. Grain-Boundary Stabilization

- Diammonium groups interact strongly with the inorganic framework at grain boundaries.
- These interactions mitigate ion migration and suppress charge recombination at grain-boundary regions.
- The rigid piperazine ring contributes to the formation of a dense, Dion–Jacobson-type interfacial skin.

---

## 5. Interfacial and Device-Level Effects

- **Enhanced interfacial contact:**  
  PiPDI forms a stable and uniform interface between the perovskite absorber and the electron transport layer (C₆₀), improving charge-extraction efficiency.
- **Improved film stability:**  
  The PiPDI overlayer protects perovskite films from environmental factors such as moisture and oxygen, slowing degradation.

---

## 6. Overall Functional Summary

PiPDI combines a rigid monocyclic piperazine dication with two iodide anions, enabling:  
- Chelation of under-coordinated Pb²⁺/Sn²⁺ and iodide vacancies by two closely spaced protonated secondary amines.  
- Formation of a dense, grain-boundary-stabilizing Dion–Jacobson-type skin.  
- Back-filling of halide defects and pinning of mobile ions through iodide-assisted hydrogen-bond networks.
