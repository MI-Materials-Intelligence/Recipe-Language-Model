Compound: VBETS (L-valine benzyl ester p-toluenesulfonate; C₁₉H₂₅NO₅S)

1. Functional Overview  
Functional Structure of This Compound  
- Ionic composition: [L-Valine benzyl ester – NH₃⁺] · [p-toluenesulfonate⁻ (TsO⁻)]  
- Functional groups:  
  - **–NH₃⁺ (protonated α-amino):** electrostatically anchors to under-coordinated Pb²⁺ and halide vacancies, forming N–H···I hydrogen bonds that suppress nonradiative recombination. The chiral L-valine configuration induces oriented crystal growth, improving carrier mobility.  
  - **Benzyl ester (–COOCH₂Ph):** hydrophobic and π–π interactive unit providing moisture resistance, interfacial compactness, and mechanical robustness; flexible ester linkage buffers thermal and lattice strain.  
  - **p-Toluenesulfonate (TsO⁻):** a non-halide anion with –SO₃⁻ coordination sites that electrostatically neutralize Pb²⁺-related defects and pin mobile ions, thereby suppressing ion migration and aiding crystallization.  
- Structural behavior: VBETS acts as a multifunctional organic salt capable of dual cation–anion passivation. Through the cooperation of –NH₃⁺, –C=O, and –SO₃⁻ groups, it chemically heals multiple defect types and simultaneously forms a hydrophobic, mechanically stable interfacial layer.

2. Detailed Functional Analysis  
Function: As passivation  

Mechanism of Action:  
- **Multi-defect cooperative passivation:**  
  The molecule offers multiple anchoring sites:  
  - –NH₃⁺ interacts with halide vacancies (V_I) and under-coordinated Pb²⁺ via hydrogen bonding and electrostatic attraction.  
  - –C=O (carbonyl) and –S=O (sulfonate) groups coordinate to Pb²⁺-related defects, forming stable Lewis adducts that eliminate deep traps.  
  - TsO⁻ compensates positively charged surface defects and immobilizes mobile ions, mitigating ion migration[1].  
- **Energy-level regulation and field modulation:**  
  VBETS-modified perovskites exhibit an upward Fermi-level shift, establishing a beneficial back-surface electric field that enhances built-in potential and promotes electron extraction. This minimizes energy loss at the perovskite/transport-layer interface.  
- **Crystallization and structural control:**  
  The sulfonate anion aids nucleation and grain growth, leading to enlarged, uniform grains with lower boundary recombination. The L-valine chiral structure contributes to lattice ordering and improved carrier mobility.  
- **Hydrophobic protection and mechanical stabilization:**  
  The benzyl-ester moiety forms a dense, hydrophobic shell that impedes H₂O/O₂ ingress. The π–π stacking among aromatic groups densifies the surface passivation network, while the flexible ester linkage absorbs strain, preventing film cracking under thermal cycling.  

Impact on Device Performance:  
- **Voc:** significantly enhanced due to deep-level trap reduction and improved energy alignment.  
- **FF:** improved by lower interfacial recombination and enhanced carrier transport.  
- **Jsc:** maintained or slightly increased as a result of reduced nonradiative losses and improved crystal orientation.  
- **Stability:** notably increased under moisture, heat, and continuous illumination due to the hydrophobic surface and ion-pinning sulfonate network.

Key Characterization Evidence:  
XRD:  
- **Main 3D perovskite peaks** preserved, with **narrowed FWHM** of (100)/(110) reflections indicating improved crystallinity and reduced defect-induced strain.  
- **PbI₂ (≈12.6°)** peak intensity **diminished**, consistent with passivation of residual PbI₂ and surface defect healing.  
- Absence of additional impurity peaks confirms successful incorporation of VBETS without phase decomposition; subtle intensity modulation suggests enhanced grain orientation.
