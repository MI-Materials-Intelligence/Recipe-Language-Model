# Compound: DMACPA  
*(4-(2,7-dibromo-9,9-dimethylacridin-10(9H)-yl)butylphosphonic acid)*

---

## 1. Functional Overview  

**Structure & Key Functional Elements**  
- **π-core:** 9,9-dimethylacridine with 2,7-dibromo substitution  
  - Conjugated aromatic core with HOMO suitable for local p-doping.  
  - 9,9-dimethyl steric bulk prevents aggregation during crystallization.  
  - Bromine substituents tune electronic structure and sterics at GBs.  

- **Anchoring group:** Phosphonic acid (–PO(OH)₂)  
  - Protonated in precursor; deprotonates during antisolvent quench.  
  - Coordinates Pb²⁺ / Pb–I polyiodides at **grain boundaries (GBs)** and the **buried bottom interface** via Pb–O–P bonding.  

- **C4 butyl linker:**  
  - Allows “molecule extrusion” during crystallization.  
  - Prevents lattice insertion while enabling segregation to GBs and bottom interface.  

**Interfacial Energy & Buried-Wetting Behavior**  
- DMACPA does **not** act as a pre-coated SAM.  
- Instead, during crystallization, it self-segregates to the buried interface and GBs, forming a **molecularly graded interfacial region**.  
- This local environment tunes nucleation behavior and suppresses interfacial defects through:  
  - **Pb–O–P coordination**  
  - **Local p-doping** near ITO  
  - **Steric stabilization** of early nuclei  

---

## 2. Detailed Functional Analysis  

### (a) Chemisorption: Pb–O–P Coordination  
- Phosphonic acid deprotonates during CB quench.  
- Forms strong **Pb–O–P** bonds at GBs and bottom interface.  
- Effects:  
  - Passivates undercoordinated Pb²⁺ → fewer SRH centers.  
  - Stabilizes emerging nuclei, smoothing crystallization.  
  - Facilitates **charge transfer**, producing local p-doping[1].

### (b) Interface Formation  
- DMACPA creates a **graded interface** comprising:  
  - Pure bulk grains  
  - DMACPA-decorated GBs  
  - A DMACPA-enriched perovskite/ITO buried contact  
- The acridine core:  
  - Aligns with perovskite VB for efficient hole extraction  
  - Generates favorable band bending at buried interface  
  - Converts perovskite/ITO from Schottky-like to **contact-matched p-contact**[2]

### (c) Effect on Wetting, Nucleation Density & Crystallization  

**Crystallization Pathway (DMACPA Present)**  
- Upon antisolvent quenching:  
  - DMACPA segregates to GBs and bottom interface.  
  - Phosphonic acid deprotonates → forms Pb–O–P anchors.  
- These interactions enable:  
  - **Earlier burst nucleation** (lower nucleation barrier)  
  - **More homogeneous nucleation** at buried interface  
  - Reduced local stress and defect formation  

**Resulting Film Morphology:**  
- Higher nucleation density  
- More uniform grain size  
- Fewer pinholes and voids  
- Lower total trap density

### (d) Influence on Defect Generation & Carrier Extraction  

**Defect Suppression**  
- GB traps passivated during formation instead of post-growth.  
- Steric acridine prevents over-accumulation/clustering.  
- Buried-interface recombination strongly suppressed.

**Carrier Extraction**  
- Local p-doping near ITO improves hole extraction.  
- DMACPA-decorated GBs provide benign transport pathways.  
- Uniform band bending reduces interfacial resistance.  

**Overall Effects:**  
- Reduced nonradiative recombination  
- Improved charge collection  
- Enhanced Voc and FF stability

### (e) Impact on Device Performance  
- **Voc ↑** (reduced nonradiative loss at GBs/buried interface)  
- **FF ↑** (contact-matched hole transport)  
- **Jsc ↔/↑** (better coverage, fewer shunts)  
- **PCE ↑** with improved uniformity and reproducibility

## 3. In-situ PL Interpretation  

### (a) Burst Nucleation Timing  
**Observation:**  
- DMACPA shifts PL onset to **earlier annealing times**.  
- PL intensity rises sharply and more synchronously.

**Mechanistic Interpretation:**  
- Pb–O–P coordination stabilizes early Pb–I clusters.  
- Segregation to buried interface promotes **early nucleation**.  
- Steric design prevents aggregation → uniform crystallization front[3].

**Classification:**  
- **Earlier nucleation burst → faster crystallization, fewer defects.**

### (b) PL Decay Slope  
**Observation:**  
- DMACPA-treated films exhibit **slow PL decay**.  

**Interpretation:**  
- Strong suppression of deep traps at GBs.  
- Benign buried interface with reduced nonradiative recombination.  
- Local p-doping enhances extraction without excessive quenching.  

**Classification:**  
- **Slow PL decay → low defect density, radiative recombination dominance.**

---

# Summary  
DMACPA is a **segregating acridine–phosphonic acid molecular dopant** that enriches grain boundaries and the buried interface, enabling **early, synchronous nucleation** and **strong defect suppression** through Pb–O–P coordination and local p-doping. In-situ PL shows **earlier nucleation burst** and **slow decay**, consistent with a film of **high crystallinity and low trap density**.
